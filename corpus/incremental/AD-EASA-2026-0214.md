# AD-EASA-2026-0214 — HPC Stage 1-4 Blade Tip Liberation: Repetitive Borescope and Blade Set Replacement

**Document Type:** Airworthiness Directive (Emergency)  
**Issuer:** European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA)  
**Effective Date:** 2026-10-27  
**Affected Engine Models:** CFM56-7B27  
**Affected Subsystems:** HPC  
**Compliance Interval:** Every 50 cycles; within 10 cycles for engines with an active HPC trend alert  
**Supersedes:** AD-EASA-2024-0312  
**Related Documents:** AD-EASA-2024-0312, SB-CFM-56-72-0193, AMM-CFM56-7B-72-00-00-200-801, AMM-CFM56-72-00-00-100-801, AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001

## Summary

Emergency AD superseding AD-EASA-2024-0312. It follows two in-flight shutdowns on CFM56-7B27 engines caused by liberation of HPC stage 3 blade tips that had cracked from leading edge erosion notches. The AD extends the inspection scope from stages 3-5 to stages 1-5, reduces the repetitive interval from 100 to 50 cycles, withdraws the 200-cycle extended interval, and requires engines with an active HPC trend alert to be inspected within 10 cycles. Engines with any blade at reject limits on stages 1-4 must have the complete **HPC blade set stage 1-4** replaced before further flight. A compressor water wash per SB-CFM-56-72-0193 becomes mandatory after each inspection.

CFM56-5B4 engines are not affected. Spare CFM56-7B27 engines and HPC modules must be inspected before installation.

## Unsafe Condition

Fatigue cracks initiating at leading edge erosion notches on HPC stage 1-4 blades can propagate to tip liberation. The liberated fragments damage downstream HPC stages and can cause a compressor surge, an in-flight shutdown and, if several stages are damaged, an uncontained failure. The previous 100-cycle interval in AD-EASA-2024-0312 did not detect the cracks in the two events, because the cracks initiated on stages 3 and 4 below the 0.9 mm erosion threshold and grew to critical length in fewer than 100 cycles.

## Required Actions

1. Within 5 days of the effective date, review engine condition trend monitoring data per AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001 for every affected engine. An engine has an active HPC trend alert if any of the following has been raised in the last 200 cycles and not closed by an inspection:
   - HPC exit temperature (T30) rise of 5°C or more from baseline;
   - core speed (Nc / N2) rise of 0.6% or more at constant thrust;
   - EGT margin loss of 8°C or more that did not recover by at least 2°C after the last wash;
   - HPC identified as a driver subsystem with drift beyond 1.5σ.
2. Perform a borescope inspection of HPC stages 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 per AMM-CFM56-7B-72-00-00-200-801, using the measurement-capable rigid borescope through ports S1 to S5.
3. Inspect every blade in each stage; record the blade count observed.
4. Apply the limits in the table below. Pay particular attention to the leading edge at 25-40% span, where the cracks in both events initiated.
5. If any blade on stages 1 to 4 is at reject limits, remove the engine from service and replace the complete HPC blade set stage 1-4 before further flight. Replacement of individual blades on stages 1 to 4 is not permitted under this AD.
6. If any blade on stage 5 is at reject limits, replace the stage 5 blades per AMM 72-31-00 before further flight.
7. After each inspection with no reject finding, perform a two-phase compressor water wash per SB-CFM-56-72-0193 and AMM-CFM56-72-00-00-100-801 before the next revenue flight, and record EGT margin before and after the wash.
8. If the post-wash EGT margin recovery is less than 2°C, reduce the next inspection interval to 25 cycles.

## Inspection Limits

| Feature | Stages | Serviceable | Reject |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leading edge erosion depth | 1-2 | Up to 0.6 mm | Greater than 0.6 mm |
| Leading edge erosion depth | 3-5 | Up to 0.7 mm | Greater than 0.7 mm |
| Erosion notch (local depth step) | 1-5 | None | Any notch with a sharp root |
| Tip curl | 1-5 | Up to 0.5 mm | Greater than 0.5 mm |
| Cracks | 1-5 | None | Any, any orientation |
| Missing tip material | 1-5 | None | Any |
| Stage 5 tip clearance | 5 | 0.6 mm or greater | Less than 0.6 mm |

The erosion limits are tighter than those in AD-EASA-2024-0312 (previously 1.2 mm on stages 3-5) because the investigation showed cracks initiating from notches at erosion depths between 0.7 and 0.9 mm.

## Background and Technical Details

EASA issues this Emergency Airworthiness Directive following two in-flight shutdowns on CFM56-7B27 engines in September 2026. In both events, an HPC stage 3 blade released its outer 20% of span. The fragments damaged stages 4 to 9 and caused a compressor surge from which the engine did not recover. Both aircraft landed safely on the remaining engine.

The investigation found that both blades had fatigue cracks initiating at erosion notches on the leading edge at 30-35% span. The erosion depth at the notch was 0.8 mm in one case and 0.75 mm in the other — below the 0.9 mm reduced-interval threshold and the 1.2 mm reject limit of AD-EASA-2024-0312. Both engines had passed an AD-EASA-2024-0312 inspection 70 and 85 cycles before the event. Crack growth analysis shows that, once a notch forms, a crack can grow from initiation to tip release in 60 to 90 cycles under high-cycle short-haul operation.

Both engines had been flagged by their operators' trend monitoring programmes. In the 300 cycles before each event, HPC exit temperature (T30) had risen by 6-7°C, core speed (Nc) by 0.8%, and EGT margin had fallen by 11°C with poor recovery after washing. In one case the engine was in the operator's critical risk tier with HPC as the single driver subsystem. EASA has therefore linked the compliance time to trend monitoring status: engines with an active HPC alert, or in the critical or warning tier with HPC as a driver subsystem, must be inspected within 10 cycles.

The requirement to replace the complete stage 1-4 blade set, rather than individual blades, reflects the finding that blades on the same stage had similar erosion. Replacing only the cracked blade would leave blades with notches close to crack initiation, and would also require moment-weight matching that is not practical on wing. EASA recognises that this requirement will put pressure on HPC blade set availability, and has asked CFM International to prioritise supply to operators with engines in the critical tier.

## Definitions

- **Active HPC trend alert:** An alert as defined in Required Action 1 that has not been closed by an inspection meeting this AD.
- **Driver subsystem:** The subsystem to which the sensors with the largest drift are mapped, per AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001.
- **Erosion notch:** A local step in the eroded leading edge profile with a sharp root radius, acting as a stress concentration.

## Job Set-Up

WARNING: CLOSE AND TAG THE FUEL SHUT-OFF VALVE. DISCONNECT THE IGNITION LEADS AND WAIT 5 MINUTES.

CAUTION: COMBUSTOR PARTS STAY HOT FOR 90 MINUTES. KEEP A FIRE WATCH FOR THE FIRST 30 MINUTES AFTER SHUTDOWN.

1. Open the thrust reverser halves.
2. Remove the igniter plugs and ports D1-D4 plugs.
3. Install the N2 cranking adaptor.

## Procedure

1. Inspect the outer liner float-wall panels from the igniter ports: burns, cracks, panel lift, missing panels, coating loss.
2. Inspect the 24 nozzle tips: carbon build-up, swirler blockage, spray erosion marks on the bulkhead.
3. From D1-D4, inspect the inner liner and bulkhead heat shields.
4. Look for hot streaks on the HPT stage 1 vanes downstream of specific nozzles.
5. Remove, flow test and clean any nozzle above limits.

## Inspection Limits

| Feature | Serviceable | Re-inspect in 250 Cycles | Reject |
|---|---|---|---|
| Float-wall panel crack | Up to 15 mm | 15-30 mm | Greater than 30 mm or crack through panel |
| Panel burn or missing material | None | Not applicable | Any |
| Panel lift | Up to 1.0 mm | 1.0-2.0 mm | Greater than 2.0 mm |
| Nozzle swirler blockage | Up to 10% | 10-25% | Greater than 25% |
| Bulkhead heat shield crack | Up to 10 mm | Not applicable | Greater than 10 mm |

## Fault Isolation Notes

| Trend Observation (AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001) | Likely Finding |
|---|---|
| Fuel flow ratio (phi) rising, T30 stable | Nozzle coking |
| EGT spread widening | Blocked nozzle; float-wall panel damage |
| phi rising with HPC drift | Combined HPC and combustor — do both inspections |
| W31 rising | Combustor exit pattern affecting HPT cooling — check HPT |

In the PW4077D fleet, the combustor appears as a driver subsystem mainly together with the HPC, and sometimes the fan. When the combustor is a driver, check the availability of the combustor liner panels and a fuel nozzle set before opening the engine, because both have been zero-stock or long-lead items.

## Service Experience

In the operator's PW4077D fleet, 62 combustor inspections over two years produced these findings:

| Finding | Share of Inspections | Typical Action |
|---|---|---|
| No defect | 48% | None |
| Nozzle coking 10-25% on one or more nozzles | 27% | Flow test and clean within 250 cycles |
| Nozzle coking above 25% | 11% | Replace nozzles before next flight |
| Float-wall panel crack 15-30 mm | 9% | Re-inspect in 250 cycles |
| Panel burn or missing material | 5% | Engine removal; combustor liner panel replacement |

Engines with phi drift above 1.5% had a coked nozzle in four inspections out of five. Engines on routes with long low-power descents had more coking than engines on short sectors, consistent with the self-cleaning effect of high nozzle temperatures at take-off and climb. Where a combustor finding was associated with a hot streak on the HPT stage 1 vanes, the follow-up HPT tip clearance check (AMM-PW4000-72-51-00-200-801) found increased shroud wear in the same sector in about half of the cases, which suggests that hot streaks also contribute to the asymmetric shroud wear addressed by AD-FAA-2023-1889.

The combustor liner and the fuel nozzle set are among the longest-lead items for the PW4077D. Planners should check stock before opening an engine flagged with the combustor as a driver subsystem.

## Close-Up

1. Install nozzles with new seals; torque per AMM 73-11-00.
2. Install plugs and igniters; reconnect leads; open the fuel valve.
3. Remove the cranking adaptor; close the thrust reverser halves.
4. Ground run: check for leaks; record EGT spread.

## Records

Record liner condition by panel, nozzle condition by position, flow test results, actions taken and EGT spread before and after.

## Background

The PW4077D combustor uses float-wall liner panels: individual cast panels attached to a cooled shell, each free to expand thermally. The design tolerates thermal cycling better than a one-piece liner, and damaged panels can be replaced individually at shop visit. The 24 fuel nozzles atomise fuel into air from the HPC. As on other engines, nozzle coking distorts the spray, produces hot streaks and accelerates panel distress. Trend monitoring of fuel flow ratio and EGT spread provides early warning, and the inspection confirms the cause.

# AD-EASA-2024-0312 — HPC Stage 3-5 Blade Visual and Borescope Inspection

**Document Type:** Airworthiness Directive  
**Issuer:** European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA)  
**Effective Date:** 2024-01-22  
**Affected Engine Models:** CFM56-7B27  
**Affected Subsystems:** HPC  
**Compliance Interval:** Every 100 cycles  
**Supersedes:** None  
**Related Documents:** SB-CFM-56-72-0193, AMM-CFM56-7B-72-00-00-200-801, AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001

## Summary

Mandatory borescope inspection of HPC stages 3, 4, and 5 blades on CFM56-7B27 engines operating in high-dust environments due to accelerated blade erosion rates. The directive establishes a repetitive 100-cycle inspection, rejection criteria for tip curl, cracking and leading edge erosion, and a stage 5 tip clearance check. Operators who can demonstrate low route particulate exposure and continuous EGT trend monitoring may apply for a 200-cycle interval.

## Applicability

This AD applies to CFM International CFM56-7B27 turbofan engines, all serial numbers, installed on Boeing 737-800 and 737-900ER aircraft, irrespective of the HPC module modification standard. Engines held as spares are affected and must be inspected before installation if more than 100 cycles have elapsed since the last qualifying inspection.

| Engine Model | Serial Numbers | Aircraft Installation | HPC Configuration |
|---|---|---|---|
| CFM56-7B27 | All | Boeing 737-800, 737-900ER | Pre- and post-SB 72-0868 |
| CFM56-7B27/3 | All | Boeing 737-800 (Tech Insertion) | All |
| CFM56-7B27E | All | Boeing 737-800 (Evolution) | All |

CFM56-5B4 engines are not affected by this AD. Although the CFM56-5B4 shares the nine-stage HPC architecture, its stage 3-5 blades use a different leading edge coating system that has not exhibited the accelerated erosion described below.

## Unsafe Condition

Leading edge erosion of HPC stage 3, 4 and 5 blades, if not detected and corrected, can progress through the erosion-resistant coating into the titanium base metal, reducing blade section thickness and introducing stress concentrations that can lead to high-cycle fatigue cracking, blade liberation, downstream HPC damage, in-flight shutdown and, in the worst case, uncontained engine failure.

## Compliance

| Condition | Initial Compliance | Repetitive Interval |
|---|---|---|
| Engines operating on high-dust routes (more than 20% of cycles to or from airports listed in Appendix 1) | Within 50 cycles after the effective date | Every 100 cycles |
| All other engines | Within 150 cycles after the effective date | Every 100 cycles |
| Engines with an approved extended-interval programme (see Alternative Methods of Compliance) | Within 150 cycles after the effective date | Every 200 cycles |

## Mandatory Actions

1. Perform borescope inspection of HPC stages 3, 4, and 5 blades per CFMI SB CFM56-7B-72-1267 Rev 3 and AMM task AMM-CFM56-7B-72-00-00-200-801.
   - Access stages 3, 4 and 5 through borescope ports S3, S4 and S5 on the HPC case at the 2 o'clock position.
   - Rotate the N2 rotor using the accessory gearbox hand-cranking pad and inspect every blade in each stage; record the blade count observed so that no blade is missed.
2. Document blade condition using digital borescope with minimum 1080p resolution; images must be retained in operator records for 2 years.
3. Reject blades with tip curling exceeding 0.8 mm, cracks in any orientation, or erosion depth exceeding 1.2 mm on the leading edge.
4. Measure stage 5 blade tip clearance per AMM Task 72-31-00-290-802; clearance below 0.6 mm requires immediate corrective action.
5. If any blade is rejected, remove the engine from service before further flight, unless a one-time ferry flight is approved by the competent authority.
6. If no blade is rejected but erosion on any blade exceeds 0.9 mm, reduce the next inspection interval to 50 cycles and perform a compressor water wash per SB-CFM-56-72-0193 to restore EGT margin before the next revenue flight.

## Safety Warnings

CAUTION: Borescope insertion through HPC access ports with engine warm may result in thermal burns. Allow minimum 45 minutes cool-down after engine shutdown before commencing inspection. Do not rotate N2 rotor manually unless aircraft has been placed on jacks and anti-rotation locks are installed.

WARNING: Make sure the engine start switch is tagged out and the ignition circuit breakers are pulled and collared before inserting any borescope probe. Inadvertent engine motoring with a probe installed will destroy the probe and can liberate fragments into the gas path.

## Terminating Action

There is no terminating action for this AD at this time. EASA is evaluating the CFM improved leading edge coating (CFM Service Bulletin 72-1301, in development) and may approve its incorporation as terminating action in a future revision or superseding AD.

## Alternative Methods of Compliance

An operator may request an extended 200-cycle interval from its National Aviation Authority if it demonstrates all of the following: (a) rolling 90-day particulate ingestion on its routes remains below ICAO Category II thresholds; (b) continuous EGT trend monitoring with automated exceedance alerts is in place per AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001; (c) HPC water wash is performed at the intervals recommended in SB-CFM-56-72-0193.

## Reporting Requirements

Report any rejected blade, and any blade with leading edge erosion greater than 0.9 mm, to CFM International Customer Support and to EASA via the Safety Data Collection and Processing System within 10 days of the inspection. Include engine serial number, cycles since new, cycles since last shop visit, route region, stage and blade position, measured erosion and the borescope images.

## Background and Technical Details

The European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) issues this Airworthiness Directive following identification of accelerated HPC blade degradation on CFM56-7B27 engines operating in high-dust environments, specifically routes transiting Middle Eastern and North African regions. Particulate ingestion analysis from engine gas path debris sampling indicates aluminium oxide concentrations 3.4× higher than ICAO baseline assumptions for these routes, resulting in leading edge erosion rates approximately 2.1× the rate assumed in the original blade life limits.

Affected stages 3 through 5 are composed of titanium alloy blades with a polyurethane erosion-resistant coating applied to the leading edge. EASA has determined that at the observed erosion rates, the coating can be fully consumed within 250 cycles, after which base metal erosion begins. Stage 5 blades are additionally subject to increased tip clearance loss due to compressor rub events correlated with dust ingestion transients. Increased tip clearance degrades HPC isentropic efficiency, manifesting as elevated EGT margins and increased fuel consumption — two parameters directly measurable via ACARS data and trending in the operator's engine health monitoring programme.

This AD requires a 100-cycle borescope inspection interval to detect and remove blades before the erosion condition progresses to a structural integrity concern. The inspection scope and reject criteria are derived from CFM Service Bulletin SB CFM56-7B-72-1267 Revision 3, which operators should have already incorporated into their approved maintenance programme. Operators maintaining engines exclusively on routes where rolling 90-day particulate ingestion remains below ICAO Category II thresholds may apply to their national authority for an extended inspection interval of 200 cycles, provided they demonstrate continuous EGT trend monitoring with automated exceedance alerts.

Two operator reports prompted this action. In the first, a CFM56-7B27 removed for an unrelated oil system fault was found at the shop to have 14 stage 4 blades with base metal erosion of up to 1.6 mm and a stage 4 blade with a 4 mm crack initiating at the eroded leading edge at 35% span. In the second, a routine borescope following an EGT margin alert found tip curl on stage 5 blades consistent with a rub against the HPC case abradable lining. Both engines had accumulated between 1,800 and 2,400 cycles since their last HPC refurbishment, and both operators flew more than 40% of their cycles to airports in the Arabian Peninsula.

EASA has also considered the interaction between erosion and HPC fouling. Fouling by fine dust and salt deposits increases surface roughness and reduces HPC efficiency in a way that is recoverable by washing, while erosion removes material and is not recoverable. Trend monitoring cannot easily distinguish the two because both raise HPC exit temperature and EGT. This is why the AD couples the borescope inspection with the water wash programme in SB-CFM-56-72-0193: if EGT margin does not recover after a wash, the residual loss is likely to be erosion or tip clearance growth, and an inspection is warranted regardless of the cycle count.

## Engine Health Monitoring Indicators

The following trend indicators, available from ACARS or quick access recorder data, are associated with the HPC degradation addressed by this AD. They map to the engine sensors used in condition monitoring as described in AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001.

| Indicator | Sensor / Station | Typical Signature of HPC Erosion |
|---|---|---|
| HPC exit total temperature | T30 | Rising trend of 3-6°C over 200 cycles at constant thrust |
| HPC exit static pressure | Ps30 | Gradual fall at constant N2 |
| Core speed | N2 (Nc) and corrected core speed (NRc) | Rising N2 required for the same thrust |
| Fuel flow ratio | phi (Wf/Ps30) | Increase of 1-2% |
| HPC bleed enthalpy | htBleed | Rising trend |
| EGT margin | EGT / T50 trend | Loss of more than 1.5°C per 100 cycles |

## Definitions

- **Cycle:** One engine start, take-off, landing and shutdown sequence.
- **Tip curl:** Permanent deformation of the blade tip, usually caused by a rub against the case lining.
- **High-dust route:** A route to or from an airport listed in Appendix 1 of this AD where the annual mean PM10 concentration exceeds 150 µg/m³.
- **Qualifying inspection:** A borescope inspection performed to the standard of this AD with records retained.

# AMM-CFM56-72-00-00-100-801 — Engine Motoring Water Wash (Compressor Performance Recovery)

**Document Type:** Engine Maintenance Manual Task  
**Publisher:** CFM International  
**Effective Date:** 2024-02-14  
**Affected Engine Models:** CFM56-7B27, CFM56-5B4  
**Affected Subsystems:** HPC, LPC  
**Task Interval:** Per SB-CFM-56-72-0193 — every 200 cycles (tropical, coastal or high-dust operation) or 400 cycles (temperate), and when EGT margin loss exceeds 8°C  
**Supersedes:** AMM-CFM56-72-00-00-100-801 Revision 37  
**Related Documents:** SB-CFM-56-72-0193, AD-EASA-2024-0312, AMM-CFM56-7B-72-00-00-200-801, AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001

## Task Summary

This task gives the procedure for an on-wing motoring water wash of the CFM56-7B27 and CFM56-5B4 compressors. The wash removes salt, sulphate and carbon deposits from the booster (LPC) and high pressure compressor (HPC) airfoils and recovers EGT margin and fuel burn. The two-phase sequence — wash followed by desalination rinse and dry run — is the standard introduced by SB-CFM-56-72-0193.

## Job Set-Up

WARNING: MAKE SURE THAT THE AREA IN FRONT OF AND BEHIND THE ENGINE IS CLEAR. THE ENGINE WILL BE MOTORED WITH THE STARTER. KEEP PERSONNEL OUT OF THE INLET HAZARD AREA DURING MOTORING.

CAUTION: DO NOT WASH AN ENGINE THAT IS HOT. LET THE ENGINE COOL FOR A MINIMUM OF 30 MINUTES AFTER SHUTDOWN. COLD WATER ON A HOT CASE CAN CAUSE THERMAL SHOCK AND BLADE TIP RUB.

CAUTION: DO NOT EXCEED THE STARTER DUTY CYCLE IN AMM 80-00-00. A STARTER THAT OVERHEATS CAN FAIL.

1. Record the most recent take-off EGT margin and T30 from the engine condition trend monitoring system. This is the pre-wash baseline.
2. Set the engine bleed air valves closed and the pack valves off per AMM 75-00-00 so that wash fluid does not enter the aircraft air conditioning system.
3. Isolate the engine anti-ice and the variable bleed valves per the wash configuration in AMM 75-00-00.
4. Open the fan cowl doors. Install the core drain bag set at the core exhaust and the bypass drain.
5. Install the wash manifold through the fan blades onto the spinner, with the nozzles aligned to spray into the booster inlet.
6. Connect the wash rig to the manifold. Fill the rig with deionised water and heat to 40-60°C.

## Procedure

1. Phase 1 — Wash:
   1. Motor the engine with the starter. Let N2 stabilise at the dry motoring speed, typically 18-22% N2.
   2. Spray deionised water at 3 bar for 60 seconds while motoring.
   3. Stop motoring. Let the engine soak for 5 minutes.
   4. Repeat the motoring and spray for a second 60-second cycle. Total deionised water used must be 90 litres minimum.
2. Phase 2 — Desalination rinse:
   1. Refill the rig with demineralised water.
   2. Motor the engine and spray 45 litres minimum at 3 bar.
   3. Stop motoring and let the engine drain for 10 minutes.
3. Remove the wash manifold and the drain bags. Make sure no tools or equipment remain in the inlet.
4. Restore the bleed, anti-ice and pack valve configuration.
5. Dry run:
   1. Start the engine per AMM 71-00-00 and run at idle for 3 minutes with N2 at 25% or above.
   2. Monitor EGT, N1, N2 and vibration. Shut down if any parameter is outside normal idle limits.
6. On the second flight cycle after the wash, record the take-off EGT margin and T30 and compare with the pre-wash baseline.

## Acceptance Criteria

| Parameter | Expected Result | Action if Not Met |
|---|---|---|
| EGT margin recovery | 4-6°C (minimum 2°C) | Below 2°C: HPC borescope per AMM-CFM56-7B-72-00-00-200-801 within 50 cycles |
| T30 reduction at constant thrust | 2-4°C | No reduction: review trend for erosion or tip clearance growth |
| Idle vibration after dry run | Below 1.0 units fan and core | Above limit: check for water in the core; repeat dry run |
| Effluent appearance | Grey or brown on the first wash, clear by end of rinse | Oil present: check the No. 3 bearing area for leaks |

## Fault Isolation Notes

A wash is both a maintenance action and a diagnostic test. Because fouling is recoverable and mechanical damage is not, the size of the EGT margin recovery separates the two. On CFM56-7B27 engines covered by AD-EASA-2024-0312, poor recovery is the commonest early indication of stage 3-5 erosion. On CFM56-5B4 engines, poor recovery together with a rise in booster exit temperature (T24) and fan speed (Nf) indicates LPC erosion of the kind described in AD-EASA-2024-1167 rather than HPC damage.

## Close-Up

1. Remove all drain equipment and dispose of effluent per local environmental regulations.
2. Close the fan cowl doors per AMM 71-11-00.
3. Make an entry in the engine log book and record the pre- and post-wash data in the trend monitoring system with the event code "WASH" so that the trend is re-baselined.

## Records

Record the date, aircraft registration, engine position and serial number, cycles since new, fluid type and volume, OAT, EGT margin before and after, T30 before and after, and any finding. Operators that track wash effectiveness across the fleet can use these records to adjust the wash interval: engines that recover more than 6°C at every wash may benefit from a shorter interval; engines that recover less than 2°C consistently should be considered for a shop visit.

## Background

Compressor washing is one of the most cost-effective tasks in the CFM56 maintenance programme. A typical wash costs a few hundred euros in labour and water and recovers fuel burn worth several thousand euros over the following 200 cycles, while also deferring the shop visit that EGT margin exhaustion eventually forces. The original procedure used a single phase with 60 litres of water and a 400-cycle interval. SB-CFM-56-72-0193 increased the volume, added the desalination rinse and shortened the interval for aggressive environments after back-to-back tests showed 35% better recovery.

The dry run is not optional. Water left in the rear HPC stages after a wash has been linked to pitting corrosion on stage 7-9 blades on aircraft parked for several days in humid climates. The dry run evaporates and expels the remaining water. If the aircraft cannot be run within 2 hours of the wash, motor the engine dry for 60 seconds instead and perform the run at the next opportunity.

Operators should not interpret a good wash recovery as evidence that the HPC blades are undamaged. The borescope required by AD-EASA-2024-0312 remains mandatory on CFM56-7B27 engines regardless of wash frequency, because erosion can progress underneath the fouling and only becomes visible to trend monitoring when it is already well advanced.

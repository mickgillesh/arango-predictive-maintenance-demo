## Task Summary

This task gives the borescope inspection of the four-stage CFM56 low pressure turbine (LPT): stage 1 nozzle guide vanes, stage 1-4 blades, blade tip shrouds and the honeycomb tip seals. It is the response to trend monitoring alerts where LPT exit temperature (T50) or LPT cooling bleed (W32) is a driver sensor.

## Job Set-Up

WARNING: TAG OUT THE ENGINE AND OPEN THE FADEC AND IGNITION CIRCUIT BREAKERS.

CAUTION: THE LPT STAYS HOT FOR UP TO 90 MINUTES AFTER SHUTDOWN. DO NOT REMOVE LPT PLUGS UNTIL THE CASE IS AT AMBIENT TEMPERATURE.

1. Open both thrust reverser halves.
2. Install the fan rotor turning tool. The LPT turns with the fan, so the fan is used to rotate the LPT blades past the probe.
3. Remove the LPT borescope plugs L1 to L4.

## Procedure

1. Insert the videoscope through port L1. Inspect the stage 1 nozzle guide vanes for burns, cracks, coating loss and trailing edge erosion.
2. Inspect the stage 1 blades while turning the fan slowly. Record:
   - tip shroud condition (cracks, missing interlock material, shroud curl);
   - airfoil leading and trailing edges (burns, nicks, cracks);
   - evidence of rub on the shroud knife edges.
3. Articulate the probe outward to view the honeycomb tip seal. Estimate the seal wear depth and look for rub tracks, missing honeycomb cells and oxidation.
4. Repeat for stages 2, 3 and 4 through ports L2 to L4.
5. Look for "hot streak" discoloration on stage 1 vanes; a hot streak in the LPT usually starts in the combustor. If found, do AMM-CFM56-72-41-00-200-801.

## Inspection Limits

| Feature | Stage | Serviceable | Reject |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nozzle guide vane trailing edge burn | 1 | Up to 2 mm into airfoil | Greater than 2 mm or any through-hole |
| Blade crack | 1-4 | None | Any |
| Tip shroud interlock wear | 1-4 | Up to 0.5 mm | Greater than 0.5 mm |
| Tip shroud crack | 1-4 | Up to 3 mm, not at interlock | Greater than 3 mm or at interlock |
| Honeycomb seal wear depth | 1-4 | Up to 1.0 mm | Greater than 1.5 mm; 1.0-1.5 mm re-inspect in 500 cycles |
| Missing honeycomb | 1-4 | Up to 10% of circumference | Greater than 10% |
| Blade trailing edge nicks | 1-4 | Up to 0.8 mm | Greater than 0.8 mm |

## Fault Isolation Notes

| Trend Observation (AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001) | Likely Finding | Action |
|---|---|---|
| T50 rising, W32 rising, N1 for given thrust rising | Tip seal wear, increased LPT leakage | Measure seal wear |
| T50 rising after over-temperature | Blade or vane distress | Over-temperature inspection plus this task |
| N1 vibration rising with T50 drift | Tip shroud damage, blade imbalance | Inspect shrouds; check fan trim balance |
| T50 rising with fuel flow ratio (phi) rising | Combustor hot streak affecting LPT | Combustor and LPT inspection together |

Engines in the CFM56-7B27 and CFM56-5B4 fleet that show the LPT as a secondary driver alongside the HPC often have normal LPT hardware: the higher T50 is the result of the HPC doing less work, which shifts load to the turbines. Do the HPC inspection first; if the HPC finding explains the trend, the LPT inspection can wait for the next planned opportunity.

## Close-Up

1. Remove the videoscope; check the tip.
2. Install the LPT plugs with new gaskets and high-temperature anti-seize. Torque per AMM 72-54-00.
3. Remove the fan rotor turning tool.
4. Close the thrust reverser halves.

## Records

Record the inspection reason (trend alert, over-temperature, scheduled), the findings per stage, honeycomb wear estimates, images, and the next action. Where the inspection was triggered by trend monitoring, record the driver sensors so that the outcome can be fed back to the monitoring programme.

## Background

The CFM56 LPT extracts work from the gas leaving the high pressure turbine to drive the fan and booster. It has four stages of uncooled nickel-alloy blades with interlocking tip shrouds. The shrouds carry knife edges that run against honeycomb seals in the LPT case to limit tip leakage. As the honeycomb wears, the leakage increases, the LPT extracts less work, the fan slows for a given fuel flow, and the FADEC adds fuel to restore thrust, raising EGT.

LPT deterioration is slower than HPC deterioration and seldom the main reason for a CFM56 shop visit, but it contributes to EGT margin loss and can be the deciding factor late in an engine's run. Because T50 is measured at the LPT exit and is the basis of the EGT indication, every module's deterioration is ultimately visible in T50. Separating true LPT deterioration from upstream effects depends on also looking at W32, T30 and fuel flow ratio, as described in AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001.

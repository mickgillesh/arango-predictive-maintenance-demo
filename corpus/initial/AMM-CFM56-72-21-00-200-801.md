# AMM-CFM56-72-21-00-200-801 — Fan Blade Removal, Fluorescent Penetrant Inspection and Dovetail Lubrication

**Affected Engine Models:** CFM56-7B27, CFM56-5B4  
**Affected Subsystems:** fan  
**Task Interval:** Every 300 cycles per AD-FAA-2024-1042; on condition after bird strike or FOD

**Related Documents:** AD-FAA-2024-1042, SB-CFM-56-72-0228, AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001

## Task Summary

This task gives the procedure to remove the fan blades from a CFM56-7B27 or CFM56-5B4 engine on wing, inspect the blade dovetails by fluorescent penetrant inspection (FPI), measure the blade chord, inspect the fan disc slots, renew the dovetail dry-film lubricant and spacers, and reinstall the blades. It is the accomplishment procedure for AD-FAA-2024-1042 and provides access for the fan frame strut inspection in SB-CFM-56-72-0228.

## Job Set-Up

WARNING: MAKE SURE THE ENGINE IS SHUT DOWN AND TAGGED OUT AND THAT THE FAN IS LOCKED WITH THE FAN ROTOR LOCK. A WINDMILLING FAN CAN CAUSE SERIOUS INJURY.

WARNING: WEAR CUT-RESISTANT GLOVES (ANSI/ISEA 105 LEVEL A4) WHEN YOU HANDLE FAN BLADES. THE LEADING AND TRAILING EDGES ARE SHARP.

1. Install the fan rotor lock.
2. Remove the spinner front cone and rear cone with the spinner cone removal tool.
3. Mark each blade and its disc slot with a temporary marker so that blades can be returned to the same slot.
4. Record the blade serial number for each slot.

## Procedure

1. Blade removal:
   1. Remove the blade retainer and the retaining clip from blade 1.
   2. Slide blade 1 forward out of its slot using the CFM blade removal tool CFM56-T-1423. Remove the spacer.
   3. Put the blade in the storage rack in its slot position.
   4. Repeat for all blades.
2. Cleaning: clean the dovetails with approved solvent and a soft brush. Remove all old dry-film lubricant with the approved stripper.
3. FPI of the dovetails:
   1. Apply level 3 penetrant to the dovetail pressure faces, suction faces and fillets. Dwell 20 minutes.
   2. Remove excess penetrant, apply level 2 developer, and let it develop for 10 minutes.
   3. Inspect under UV-A light after a minimum 20-minute dark adaptation.
   4. Record any indication with its length and location.
4. Chord measurement: measure the chord of each blade at 50% span with the digital caliper and chord adaptor. Reject below 263.4 mm.
5. Leading edge: inspect from root to tip for impact damage, erosion and tears. Reject damage deeper than 1.5 mm in the 0-75% span zone.
6. Fan disc slots: inspect each slot for fretting, galling, coating loss and cracks. Reject the disc if galling or cracks are present; refer to the shop for coating loss over 25% of the slot pressure face.
7. Fan frame strut access: where SB-CFM-56-72-0228 is being accomplished, carry out the eddy current inspection of the strut trailing edge fillets at positions 4 and 5 now, while the blades are out.
8. Lubrication: apply a uniform coat of dry-film lubricant CP8013 to the dovetail pressure faces of each serviceable blade. Cure per the product data sheet.
9. Installation:
   1. Install a new spacer 1574M79P03 in each slot.
   2. Slide each blade into its original slot.
   3. Install a new retaining clip 1574M78G01. Torque the retainer to 45 Nm ± 2 Nm.

## Inspection Limits

| Feature | Serviceable | Reject |
|---|---|---|
| FPI linear indication, dovetail faces | None, or rounded indication up to 0.25 mm | Linear indication greater than 0.5 mm; any indication in fillet |
| Corrosion pit, dovetail pressure face | Up to 0.25 mm depth, blended | Greater than 0.25 mm depth |
| Chord at 50% span | 263.4 mm or more | Less than 263.4 mm |
| Leading edge damage, 0-75% span | Up to 1.5 mm depth | Greater than 1.5 mm |
| Leading edge damage, 75-100% span | Up to 3.0 mm depth, blended | Greater than 3.0 mm |
| Disc slot fretting | Polished, no material transfer | Galling or cracks |

## Fault Isolation Notes

| Trend Observation (AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001) | Possible Fan Cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| N1 vibration step change after a flight | Blade damage (bird, FOD), lost spacer | Unscheduled fan inspection per this task |
| Gradual N1 vibration increase | Uneven lubricant wear, blade shifted in slot | Inspect at next opportunity; re-lubricate; trim balance |
| Fan speed (Nf/NRf) rising for the same thrust, BPR falling | Leading edge erosion of the fan | Chord measurement; consider blade repair |
| No trend change | Cracks are not visible to trend monitoring | Rely on the scheduled AD-FAA-2024-1042 interval |

## Close-Up

1. Remove the fan rotor lock.
2. Turn the fan by hand and make sure all blades are seated and move freely.
3. Install the spinner rear and front cones. Torque per AMM 72-21-00.
4. If any blade was replaced or moved, do a fan trim balance per AMM 71-00-00-750-801 and record the balance weights.
5. Do an engine ground run at idle and check N1 vibration is below 1.0 unit.

## Records

Record the task reference and AD-FAA-2024-1042 compliance; the serial number and slot of each blade; FPI results; chord measurements; leading edge findings; disc slot condition; lubricant batch number; and the trim balance result. Report any crack indication to CFM and the FAA within 72 hours as required by the AD.

## Background

The CFM56 fan is the largest rotating component in the engine and produces about 80% of the thrust. Its blades are solid titanium alloy (Ti-6Al-4V) with a mid-span shroud on the CFM56-5B and a wide-chord design on the CFM56-7B. Each blade is held in the disc by a dovetail root that slides axially into a slot. The dovetail carries the full centrifugal load of the blade — around 50 tonnes at take-off speed for a CFM56-7B blade — and a thin dry-film lubricant layer lets the blade seat and slide slightly as the disc grows under load.

When the lubricant wears through, metal-to-metal fretting begins, and the fretting debris holds moisture against the blade surface. AD-FAA-2024-1042 was issued after two fan blade fractures that started from corrosion pits formed in exactly this way. Regular renewal of the lubricant at the 300-cycle inspection removes the cause. The same blade removal also gives the only practical on-wing access to the inner fillets of the fan frame struts covered by SB-CFM-56-72-0228, so operators normally plan the two tasks together.

# AD-EASA-2023-1423 — HPC Stage 6-8 Labyrinth Seal Inspection and Replacement

**Document Type:** Airworthiness Directive  
**Issuer:** European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA)  
**Effective Date:** 2023-12-01  
**Affected Engine Models:** PW4077D, V2527-A5  
**Affected Subsystems:** HPC  
**Compliance Interval:** At every HPC module removal, not to exceed 4,000 cycles  
**Supersedes:** None  
**Related Documents:** AMM-PW4000-72-00-00-200-801, AMM-V2500-72-00-00-200-802, SB-IAE-V2500-A5-72-0103, AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001

## Summary

Mandatory dimensional inspection of HPC stages 6-8 labyrinth seal knife edges and seal lands on PW4000-series (PW4077D) and V2527-A5 engines, following identification of progressive seal clearance growth causing measurable SFC degradation. Where module removal is not scheduled before the 4,000-cycle limit, a calibrated in-situ borescope measurement is accepted.

## Applicability

| Engine Model | Aircraft | HPC Seal Configuration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| PW4077D (PW4000-112 series) | Boeing 777-200 | Stage 6-8 rotating knife-edge seals with abradable lands | All serial numbers |
| Other PW4000-112 series | Boeing 777-200/-300 | Same seal design | All serial numbers |
| V2527-A5 | Airbus A320-232 | Stage 6-8 rotating knife-edge seals | Pre- and post-SB V2500-72-0598 |

## Unsafe Condition

Excessive HPC interstage seal clearance reduces HPC efficiency and stall margin. On engines with clearances well beyond limits, a compressor stall during a rapid acceleration — for example a go-around — has been shown in analysis to be possible. Seal backing ring cracks, if undetected, can lead to liberation of seal segments into the gas path and secondary damage to the HPC rear stages.

## Compliance

| Condition | Action |
|---|---|
| HPC module removed for any reason | Perform Mandatory Actions 1-5 before module reassembly |
| HPC module not removed by 4,000 cycles since last seal inspection | Perform in-situ borescope measurement per Mandatory Action 6 before 4,000 cycles; remove module if any measurement is outside limits |
| Engines that already exceed 4,000 cycles since last seal inspection on the effective date | Within 300 cycles after the effective date |

## Mandatory Actions

1. At each HPC module removal, visually and dimensionally inspect labyrinth seal knife edges on HPC stages 6, 7, and 8 rotating seals per manufacturer's CMM.
2. Measure knife edge height using calibrated depth gauge; reject if height is below 1.2 mm.
3. Measure seal land clearance per Table 3 of this AD; replace seal if land clearance exceeds 1.8 mm.
4. Inspect seal backing ring for cracks or deformation; reject if any crack is present.
5. Document all measurements and retain records for the life of the engine module.
6. Where module removal is not scheduled, perform an in-situ borescope measurement per AMM-PW4000-72-00-00-200-801 (PW4077D) or AMM-V2500-72-00-00-200-802 (V2527-A5), using a borescope calibrated to ±0.1 mm.

## Inspection Limits (Table 3)

| Feature | Stage | New | Serviceable Limit | Reject |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Knife edge height | 6, 7, 8 | 1.65 mm | 1.2 mm or more | Less than 1.2 mm |
| Knife edge tip thickness | 6, 7, 8 | 0.15 mm | Up to 0.35 mm (mushrooming) | Greater than 0.35 mm |
| Seal land radial clearance | 6 | 0.45 mm | Up to 1.8 mm | Greater than 1.8 mm |
| Seal land radial clearance | 7, 8 | 0.50 mm | Up to 1.8 mm | Greater than 1.8 mm |
| Abradable coating loss | 6, 7, 8 | None | Up to 25% of circumference | Greater than 25% or substrate exposed |
| Backing ring cracks | 6, 7, 8 | None | None | Any |

## Safety Warnings

NOTE: In-situ borescope inspection of labyrinth seals provides only limited visibility. Where a borescope inspection is performed in lieu of physical measurement, operators must ensure the borescope system has been calibrated to provide dimensional measurements accurate to ±0.1 mm. If any doubt exists about the condition of a seal, the module must be removed for physical inspection.

CAUTION: Seal knife edges are sharp and easily damaged. Handle rotor assemblies with knife-edge guards installed.

## Terminating Action

None. The inspection remains repetitive. Installation of the improved abradable coating referenced in the manufacturer service bulletins reduces the rate of clearance growth but does not terminate the inspection requirement.

## Reporting Requirements

Report every seal rejected for clearance greater than 2.2 mm, and every backing ring crack, to EASA via the Safety Data Collection and Processing System within 30 days.

## Background and Technical Details

EASA issues this Airworthiness Directive to address progressive deterioration of HPC labyrinth seals on PW4000 and V2527-A5 engines, following identification of seal land clearance growth at a rate exceeding design predictions on engines accumulating more than 3,500 cycles. The HPC labyrinth seals control interstage leakage that, if excessive, reduces HPC isentropic efficiency and requires higher fuel flow to maintain the required engine pressure ratio for a given thrust level.

Labyrinth seal degradation on both engine types is associated with the periodic thermal cycling between take-off maximum thrust and idle, which produces differential thermal growth between the rotating knife edges and the stationary abradable seal lands. Over many cycles, the abradable coating is worn away, increasing the running clearance. On engines examined post-removal, stage 6-8 seal clearances exceeding 1.8 mm were found in approximately 12% of cases, corresponding to a 2.1% increase in specific fuel consumption compared to engines with new seals.

The inspection intervals in this AD are designed to detect seals approaching the rejection limit before the operational fuel penalty becomes significant. The 4,000-cycle hard limit applies regardless of module removal schedule; operators should plan HPC overhaul visits to coincide with this limit to minimise maintenance disruption. EASA notes that both PW4000 and V2527-A5 operators may benefit from the SilcoTec abradable coating upgrade referenced in the respective manufacturer service bulletins, which reduces seal land wear rates by approximately 35% and can extend the seal life between replacements.

Seal leakage has a distinctive trend monitoring signature, described in AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001. Air leaking backwards across the seals is re-compressed, so the HPC must work harder: HPC exit temperature (T30) rises and HPC exit static pressure (Ps30) falls at constant core speed, while bleed enthalpy (htBleed) rises because the bleed air is hotter. Unlike fouling, this deterioration does not recover after a compressor wash. Operators who see this pattern on V2527-A5 or PW4077D engines approaching 3,500 cycles since their last seal inspection should bring the in-situ measurement forward.

On the V2527-A5, the same shop visit that addresses seals is the natural point to assess HPC stage 5-7 blade platforms for the repair described in SB-IAE-V2500-A5-72-0103, because the blades must be removed from the drum to measure the knife edges.

| HPC exit static pressure | Ps30 | Falling at constant N2 |
| HPC exit total pressure | P30 | Falling |

## Definitions

- **Labyrinth seal:** A non-contacting seal formed by rotating knife edges running close to a stationary land.
- **Abradable coating:** A soft coating on the seal land into which the knife edges can cut a track without damage.
- **Mushrooming:** Flattening and widening of a knife edge tip after rubs.

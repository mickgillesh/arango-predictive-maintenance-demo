# AD-FAA-2023-1889 — HPT Shroud Clearance Measurement and Segment Replacement

**Document Type:** Airworthiness Directive  
**Issuer:** Federal Aviation Administration (FAA)  
**Effective Date:** 2023-09-22  
**Affected Engine Models:** PW4077D  
**Affected Subsystems:** HPT  
**Compliance Interval:** At next HPT inspection not to exceed 1,500 cycles from effective date  
**Supersedes:** None  
**Related Documents:** AMM-PW4000-72-51-00-200-801, AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001

## Summary

Mandatory HPT Stage 1 blade tip clearance measurement on PW4000-112 series engines, including the PW4077D, following three blade tip rub events caused by asymmetric shroud segment wear on engines transferred to medium-haul high-cycle operations. Shroud segments with wear grooves deeper than 0.3 mm must be replaced, and gas path debris sampling is required.

## Applicability

| Engine Model | Aircraft | HPT Configuration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| PW4077D | Boeing 777-200 | Two-stage HPT, segmented stage 1 shroud | All serial numbers |
| PW4084D, PW4090 | Boeing 777-200ER/-300 | Same stage 1 shroud | All serial numbers |

## Unsafe Condition

Asymmetric HPT stage 1 shroud wear produces non-uniform blade tip clearance. Under high-power operation, blades can rub against the most worn sector, causing tip cracking and tip liberation. Blade tip liberation can cause downstream turbine damage and, in combination with other failures, an uncontained engine failure.

## Compliance

| Condition | Action |
|---|---|
| Engines with more than 3,000 cycles since last HPT shroud replacement | Measure within 500 cycles after the effective date |
| All other engines | Measure at next HPT inspection, not to exceed 1,500 cycles after the effective date |
| After any shroud segment replacement | Re-measure within 50 cycles |

## Mandatory Actions

1. Measure HPT stage 1 blade tip clearance at four circumferential positions (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) using the PW-approved capacitance probe fixture, per AMM-PW4000-72-51-00-200-801.
2. Calculate average clearance and reject if below 0.45 mm or if difference between maximum and minimum positions exceeds 0.25 mm (indicating ovality).
3. Replace HPT shroud segments showing wear grooves exceeding 0.3 mm depth per PW Overhaul Manual OM-72-55-04.
4. Perform gas path dust and debris sampling per PW Service Letter SL-72-218.
5. Record all clearances, the calculated average and asymmetry, and the debris sample results.

## Inspection Limits

| Measurement | Serviceable | Reject |
|---|---|---|

| Max − min clearance (asymmetry) | 0.25 mm or less | Greater than 0.25 mm |
| Shroud wear groove depth | 0.3 mm or less | Greater than 0.3 mm |
| Shroud segment crack | None | Any |

## Safety Warnings

WARNING: HPT components are subject to Foreign Object Damage (FOD) control procedures. All tools must be accounted for before and after every HPT access. Use bonded tool pouches and establish a tool count log. Loss of a tool into the gas path constitutes a mandatory report to the FAA within 24 hours.

CAUTION: Let the engine cool for a minimum of 90 minutes before inserting the capacitance probe. Probe calibration is invalid above 50°C case temperature.

## Alternative Methods of Compliance

PW4000 operators that already have approved tip clearance monitoring programmes using approved equipment may credit those readings against this AD's requirements provided the measurement technique and accuracy meet or exceed the requirements of PW-TC-S1-FIXTURE.

## Reporting Requirements

Report any rub event, any measurement below 0.35 mm average, and any blade tip crack to the FAA Engine Certification Office within 10 days.

## Background and Technical Details

The Federal Aviation Administration issues this Airworthiness Directive following identification of HPT shroud segment wear patterns on PW4000 engines that have resulted in blade tip rubs in three in-service events. The rubs originated from asymmetric shroud wear caused by variable thermal gradients during repeated rapid thrust changes typical of high-cycle short-haul profiles. While the PW4000 was designed for long-haul operations with relatively stable power settings, a significant portion of the installed fleet has been transferred to operators utilising the engine on medium-haul routes with higher cycle rates.

The primary concern is that asymmetric shroud wear, where the shroud wear depth varies by more than 0.25 mm around the circumference, creates a non-uniform tip clearance that under high-power operation can allow the blade tip to contact the shroud in the most worn sector. The contact produces a friction-induced temperature spike at the blade tip, which can initiate tip cracking and in severe cases result in blade tip liberation. Three such liberation events have been reported globally, none of which resulted in uncontained failure, but the potential for escalation exists under specific condition combinations.

Operators are required to establish a shroud clearance monitoring programme as part of their approved maintenance schedule. PW4000 operators that already have approved tip clearance monitoring programmes using approved equipment may credit those readings against this AD's requirements provided the measurement technique and accuracy meet or exceed the requirements of PW-TC-S1-FIXTURE. PW Customer Support is available to review existing operator measurement programmes for equivalency determination.

Increasing tip clearance and tip rubs both affect trend data. As clearance grows, more gas leaks over the blade tips, HPT efficiency falls and EGT rises at constant HPC exit temperature. On the PW4077D the HPT cooling bleed (W31) rises as well, because the shroud cooling flow increases when segments distort. A rub produces a small, sharp N2 vibration change. Operators should configure their trend monitoring per AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001 to flag these signatures and bring the measurement forward when they appear.

## Definitions

- **Shroud segment:** One of the arc-shaped segments forming the stationary ring around the HPT stage 1 blade tips.
- **Ovality:** Out-of-roundness of the shroud ring, measured as the difference between maximum and minimum clearance.
- **Capacitance probe:** A sensor that measures the gap to a passing blade tip by its electrical capacitance.

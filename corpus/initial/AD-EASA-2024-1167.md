# AD-EASA-2024-1167 — LPC Blade Leading Edge Erosion Measurement

**Document Type:** Airworthiness Directive  
**Issuer:** European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA)  
**Effective Date:** 2024-05-30  
**Affected Engine Models:** CFM56-5B4, V2527-A5  
**Affected Subsystems:** LPC  
**Compliance Interval:** Every 500 cycles or at A-check interval, whichever is sooner  
**Supersedes:** None  
**Related Documents:** AMM-CFM56-5B-72-22-00-200-801, AMM-CFM56-72-00-00-100-801, AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001

## Summary

Mandatory LPC Stage 1 blade chord measurement on CFM56-5B4 and V2527-A5 engines following three surge-recovery events attributed to LPC stall margin reduction from leading edge erosion beyond serviceable limits. Blades with chord below 98.2 mm at 80% span must be replaced, and a stall margin verification test is required after any replacement.

## Applicability

| Engine Model | Aircraft | LPC Configuration | LPC Stage 1 Blades |
|---|---|---|---|
| CFM56-5B4 | Airbus A320-214 | Four-stage booster | 76 blades |
| V2527-A5 | Airbus A320-232 | Four-stage LPC (stages 1.5-2.5 and 3) | 72 blades |

## Unsafe Condition

Erosion of LPC stage 1 blade leading edges reduces the LPC stall margin. Under rapid deceleration at high altitude, a reduced stall margin can lead to an engine surge, loss of thrust, and, if more than one engine is affected, a dual engine surge event. Three surge-recovery events on A320-family aircraft have been attributed to this condition.

## Compliance

| Condition | Initial Measurement | Repetitive Measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Engines operated more than 30% of cycles on routes listed in Appendix 1 (contaminated runways, volcanic ash or sand exposure) | Within 100 cycles after the effective date | Every 500 cycles or at A-check, whichever is sooner |
| All other engines | Within 500 cycles after the effective date | Every 500 cycles or at A-check |
| Engines with an approved extended programme | Within 500 cycles after the effective date | Every 750 cycles |

## Mandatory Actions

1. Remove LPC fan cowl access panels.
2. Measure LPC Stage 1 blade leading edge chord at 80% span using borescope measurement tool or physical gauge if blades are accessible, per AMM-CFM56-5B-72-22-00-200-801 (CFM56-5B4) or the equivalent IAE AMM task (V2527-A5).
3. Reject blades with chord below 98.2 mm.
4. Inspect all blades for foreign object damage with nicks or gouges exceeding 3 mm depth on leading or trailing edges.
5. Perform stall margin verification test per CFMI Test Procedure TP-56-072-04 (CFM56-5B4) or IAE TP-V25-072-11 (V2527-A5) if any blade requires replacement.
6. Report all blade replacements under this AD to the EASA Safety Data Collection and Processing System (SDCPS) within 30 days of the maintenance action.

## Inspection Limits

| Feature | Serviceable | Reject |
|---|---|---|
| Chord at 80% span | 98.2 mm or more | Less than 98.2 mm |
| Leading edge nicks/gouges | Up to 3 mm depth, blended per AMM | Greater than 3 mm |
| Trailing edge nicks | Up to 3 mm depth | Greater than 3 mm |
| Tip curl | Up to 1.0 mm | Greater than 1.0 mm |
| Cracks | None | Any |

NOTE: LPC blade replacement requires adjustment of blade moment-weight grouping to maintain balance within limits per AMM 72-22-00. Random blade replacement without moment-weight balancing will result in unacceptable N1 vibration levels.

## Background and Technical Details

EASA has identified that CFM56-5B4 and V2527-A5 engines operating on routes subject to runway construction debris, volcanic ash, or fine sand ingestion are experiencing LPC Stage 1 blade erosion at rates exceeding the original design assumptions by 60-80%. The LPC is the first compressor stage and has no filtration upstream; it is therefore the most exposed to ingested particulate matter. Erosion reduces the blade leading edge chord and changes the airfoil camber, reducing stage pressure ratio and stall margin.

A stall margin reduction of more than 3% on the LPC has been correlated with surge events at high altitudes during rapid deceleration. Three surge-recovery events on A320-family aircraft have been attributed to LPC erosion beyond the serviceable limits. Post-event borescope inspections found blade chord measurements averaging 96.8 mm against a minimum serviceable limit of 98.2 mm, a deficiency that had accumulated over approximately 600 cycles on heavily contaminated routes.

Operators are required by this AD to implement a measurement programme at the 500-cycle interval. Where operators maintain comprehensive route contamination records and can demonstrate average route contamination below the EASA defined threshold for accelerated erosion, the interval may be extended to 750 cycles with the approval of the National Aviation Authority. Operators are also required to report all blade replacements under this AD to the EASA Safety Data Collection and Processing System (SDCPS) within 30 days of the maintenance action, to support EASA's ongoing fleet-wide erosion trend analysis.

Washing does not restore eroded material. Operators sometimes see an initial recovery in LPC performance after a compressor wash per AMM-CFM56-72-00-00-100-801, because fouling and erosion occur together, but a recovery that is smaller than expected — or a rise in booster exit temperature (T24) that returns within a few dozen cycles — indicates erosion. In trend monitoring (AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001), LPC erosion appears as rising T24 and rising corrected fan speed (NRf) for a given thrust, and sometimes a small fall in bypass ratio as the booster passes less core flow.

| Indicator | Sensor / Station | Signature of LPC Erosion |
|---|---|---|

| Physical fan speed | Nf | Rising |
| Bypass ratio | BPR | Slight decrease |
| HPC exit temperature | T30 | Secondary rise as HPC inlet temperature rises |

## Definitions

- **Chord:** The straight-line distance from leading edge to trailing edge of an airfoil section.
- **Stall margin:** The margin between the compressor operating line and its stall line.
- **Booster:** The low pressure compressor of a CFM56 engine, driven by the fan shaft.

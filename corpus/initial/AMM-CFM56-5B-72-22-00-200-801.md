## Task Summary

This task gives the procedure to inspect the CFM56-5B4 booster (low pressure compressor) stage 1 blades and to measure their chord at 80% span with the LPC borescope measurement attachment. It is the accomplishment procedure for AD-EASA-2024-1167 on the CFM56-5B4.

## Job Set-Up

WARNING: TAG THE ENGINE MASTER LEVER AND OPEN THE FADEC CIRCUIT BREAKERS. WIND CAN TURN THE FAN — INSTALL THE FAN ROTOR TURNING TOOL AND KEEP CONTROL OF THE ROTOR.

1. Open the fan cowl access panels.
2. Remove the LPC borescope port plug at the 1 o'clock position.
3. Install the measurement attachment CMI-LPC-MEAS-01 on the borescope and verify it against its reference gauge.

## Procedure

1. Insert the borescope with the measurement attachment through the LPC port. Position the attachment at 80% span on the first blade.
2. Turn the fan slowly with the turning tool. Measure the chord at 80% span on each of the 76 blades.
3. Inspect each blade's leading and trailing edge for nicks, gouges and tears. Measure the depth of any damage.
4. Inspect for tip curl and cracks.
5. If the fan blades are already removed for AD-FAA-2024-1042 (AMM-CFM56-72-21-00-200-801), measure the chord directly with the digital caliper instead — the result is more accurate.
6. Compare with the limits below.
7. If any blade is rejected, replace it with a moment-weight matched blade per AMM 72-22-00 and perform the stall margin verification test TP-56-072-04.

## Inspection Limits

| Feature | Serviceable | Reject |
|---|---|---|
| Chord at 80% span | 98.2 mm or more | Less than 98.2 mm |
| Chord 98.2-99.0 mm | Serviceable; re-measure in 250 cycles | Not applicable |
| Leading or trailing edge damage | Up to 3 mm depth | Greater than 3 mm |
| Tip curl | Up to 1.0 mm | Greater than 1.0 mm |
| Cracks | None | Any |

| Trend Observation (AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001) | Likely Finding | Action |
|---|---|---|

## Service Experience

| Finding (96 measurements) | Share | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum chord ≥ 99.0 mm | 58% | — |
| Minimum chord 98.2-99.0 mm | 30% | Re-measured at 250 cycles |
| One or more blades below 98.2 mm | 8% | Replaced; stall margin test passed |
| FOD damage beyond 3 mm | 4% | Replaced |

Erosion rates were two to three times higher on aircraft operating from airports with runway works or in desert regions. Engines with LPC erosion beyond limits usually also showed HPC drift (T30, Nc), and in several cases the HPC was the leading driver subsystem in trend monitoring; the booster erosion was found when the AD-EASA-2024-1167 measurement was combined with an HPC borescope. LPC blade sets and LPC vane sets have been out of stock at times. Operators should hold moment-weight matched spare blades for single-blade replacements.

## Close-Up

1. Remove the borescope and attachment.
2. Install the port plug with a new gasket; torque per AMM 72-22-00.
3. Remove the fan rotor turning tool.
4. Close the fan cowl access panels.
5. If a blade was replaced, check N1 vibration at idle and at 70% N1 during the stall margin test.

## Records

Record AD-EASA-2024-1167 compliance; the 76 chord measurements; the minimum chord; damage findings; any replacement with blade serial numbers; and the stall margin test result. Report replacements to EASA SDCPS within 30 days.

## Background

The CFM56-5B booster has four stages and is driven by the fan shaft. Stage 1 sits directly behind the fan and receives the core airflow and everything it carries: sand, ash, runway grit and de-icing fluid residue. Its blades are thin titanium airfoils whose performance depends on a sharp leading edge. Erosion blunts and shortens the leading edge, which reduces the chord, changes the inlet angle and reduces stall margin.

AD-EASA-2024-1167 followed three surge-recovery events on A320-family aircraft. In each case, post-event inspection found stage 1 chords around 96.8 mm, well below the 98.2 mm limit. Erosion had accumulated over about 600 cycles on heavily contaminated routes. The 500-cycle measurement interval in the AD is designed to catch blades before they reach this condition. In the operator's CFM56-5B4 fleet the LPC is rarely the leading driver subsystem in trend monitoring, but it often contributes as a secondary driver alongside HPC deterioration, because the same particulate exposure affects both.

# SB-GE-GE90-72-0312 — Combustor Dome Assembly Replacement with Improved Design

**Document Type:** Service Bulletin  
**Recommended By:** GE Aviation  
**Effective Date:** 2024-03-07  
**Affected Engine Models:** GE90-115B  
**Affected Subsystems:** combustor  
**Recommended Interval:** At next combustor module removal, not to exceed 6,000 cycles  
**Supersedes:** None  
**Related Documents:** AD-FAA-2023-2156, SB-GE-GE90-72-0288

## Summary

Improved combustor dome assembly for GE90-115B engines incorporating enlarged swirler passages and enhanced film cooling, reducing dome peak temperature by 50°C under maximum continuous thrust and addressing thermal distress on ultra-high-thrust extended segments.

## Applicability

| Engine Model | Aircraft | Module |
|---|---|---|
| GE90-115B | Boeing 777-300ER, 777-200LR, 777F | Combustor |

## Recommended Actions

1. Replace combustor dome assembly with improved design P/N GE90-115-DOME-UPG-002 at next combustor module removal.
2. Improved design incorporates enlarged swirler passages and enhanced film cooling holes that reduce dome temperature by approximately 50°C under maximum continuous thrust.
3. Verify fuel nozzle alignment after dome installation per GE CMM 72-41-00.
4. Perform combustion stability ground test per GE QTR-72-103 before returning engine to service.

## Special Tools Required

- GE combustor module stand P/N GE90-CMB-STAND-002
- Fuel nozzle alignment tool P/N GE90-FN-ALIGN-004
- Combustion stability test data acquisition system, minimum 10 kHz sampling rate

## Background and Technical Details

GE Aviation issues this Service Bulletin to recommend replacement of the GE90-115B combustor dome assembly with an improved design that addresses thermal distress observed on a portion of the fleet operating extended-duration ultra-high-thrust segments. The GE90-115B powers the Boeing 777-300ER and selected 777-200LR variants, which regularly operate at near-maximum takeoff thrust for extended periods when departing from high-altitude airports in hot-and-high conditions, or when operating at maximum payload on challenging route segments such as Sydney to Dallas or Singapore to New York.

Under these demanding operating conditions, the original combustor dome experiences temperature loading that is at the upper end of its design envelope. Analysis of eight combustor domes removed from engines with more than 5,000 cycles operating primarily on ultra-long-range routes found dome distortion measurements exceeding the original manufacturing tolerance in 62% of cases, with associated cracking at the swirler-to-dome braze joints in 25% of cases. The braze joint cracking, while not immediately safety-critical due to the redundant cooling film, does reduce the cooling effectiveness and can lead to accelerated oxidation of the dome base material.

The improved dome design incorporates an enlarged swirler passage cross-section (+12% area) that reduces swirler pressure drop and improves air distribution, combined with a redesigned film cooling hole geometry that increases the cooling effectiveness of the downstream panel film by approximately 18%. Thermal analysis predicts a 50°C reduction in dome peak temperature under maximum continuous thrust conditions. GE Aviation has validated this prediction through a comparison of dome temperature measurements on an engine teststand running the original and improved domes on the same operating cycle.

Because the combustor module is removed for this upgrade, operators should check whether the HPT disc inspection of AD-FAA-2023-2156 is due and whether the LPT stage 5-7 upgrade of SB-GE-GE90-72-0288 can be combined in the same shop visit.

## Definitions

- **Dome:** The front wall of the combustor that holds the swirlers and fuel nozzles.
- **Swirler:** A device that imparts rotation to combustor inlet air to stabilise the flame.
- **Braze joint:** A joint made by melting a filler metal between two parts.

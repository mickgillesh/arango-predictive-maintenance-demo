# SB-PW-4000-72-0176 — LPC Variable Stator Vane Adjustment and Clearance Verification

**Document Type:** Service Bulletin  
**Recommended By:** Pratt & Whitney  
**Effective Date:** 2023-11-17  
**Affected Engine Models:** PW4077D  
**Affected Subsystems:** LPC  
**Recommended Interval:** At every LPC module removal  
**Supersedes:** SB-PW-4000-72-0122  
**Related Documents:** AMM-PW4000-72-31-00-200-801, AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001

## Summary

Enhanced LPC variable stator vane (VSV) rigging and trunnion bushing wear inspection procedure for PW4000-series engines, including the PW4077D, tightening the bushing wear limit from 0.25 mm to 0.15 mm and adding precision inclinometer verification, reducing LPC stall event rate by 31%.

| Engine Model | Aircraft | LPC VSV Stages | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| PW4077D | Boeing 777-200 | 2-5 | All serial numbers |
| PW4084D, PW4090 | Boeing 777-200ER/-300 | 2-5 | All serial numbers |

## Reason

A fleet survey found approximately 18% of PW4000 LPC modules returning to the shop with VSV rigging outside the specified tolerance. Worn trunnion bushings were the primary cause. Out-of-tolerance VSVs reduce LPC efficiency and stall margin, and were a contributing factor in LPC stall events recorded on the fleet. Pratt & Whitney has tightened the bushing wear limit and added an inclinometer check to the reassembly procedure.

## Recommended Actions

1. At LPC module disassembly, record the angular position of all variable stator vane (VSV) stages 2-5 before removal.
2. Inspect VSV trunnion bushings for wear exceeding 0.15 mm diameter increase from nominal.
3. Replace worn bushings with PW-approved repair specification to restore nominal clearance.
4. At reassembly, verify stagger angle matches PW rigging specification ±0.5° per stage.
5. Perform VSV rigging check per PW-72-VSV-RIG-003 after each module assembly.
6. On wing, between shop visits, perform the VSV rigging check in AMM-PW4000-72-31-00-200-801 whenever trend monitoring shows LPC exit temperature drift or after an LPC stall event.

## Background and Technical Details

Pratt & Whitney issues this Service Bulletin to recommend enhanced procedures for LPC variable stator vane (VSV) adjustment and clearance verification during module overhaul of PW4000 series engines. Variable stator vanes are a key feature of the LPC design, allowing the inlet flow angle to be optimised across the engine power range from idle to maximum takeoff. Incorrect VSV settings or excessive bushing wear, by allowing VSV positioning error, degrade LPC efficiency and stall margin. PW has identified that approximately 18% of PW4000 LPC modules returning to the shop have VSV rigging outside the specified tolerance, suggesting that the current overhaul procedure does not adequately capture all sources of VSV positioning error.

The primary source of positioning error identified is wear in the VSV trunnion bushings, which are the bearing surfaces that support the vane rotation axis in the casing. Worn bushings allow the vane angle to deviate from the actuator command position, particularly under the aerodynamic loading experienced at high engine speeds. PW has revised the bushing wear limit from 0.25 mm to 0.15 mm diameter increase from nominal, a change that would have identified the worn bushings in 73% of the out-of-tolerance assemblies identified in the fleet survey.

A one-year trial with three operators who adopted the revised limits and the inclinometer check showed a 31% reduction in LPC stall events compared with the rest of the fleet. The trial also found that on-wing rigging checks, performed when trend data first showed LPC drift, caught a further group of engines whose actuators had drifted between shop visits.

In trend monitoring (AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001), VSV mis-rigging shows as a rise in LPC exit temperature (T24) and a change in corrected fan speed for a given thrust. Unlike erosion, it can appear suddenly after maintenance that disturbed the VSV linkage. Unlike fouling, it does not recover after a wash.

## Definitions

- **Variable stator vane (VSV):** A compressor stator vane whose angle is changed in service by an actuator through a unison ring.
- **Trunnion:** The pivot of a variable stator vane, running in a bushing in the compressor case.
- **Stagger angle:** The angle between the vane chord and the engine axis.

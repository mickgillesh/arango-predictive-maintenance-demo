# AD-FAA-2024-0583 — Combustion Liner Thermal Cracking and Fuel Nozzle Inspection

**Document Type:** Airworthiness Directive  
**Issuer:** Federal Aviation Administration (FAA)  
**Effective Date:** 2024-02-09  
**Affected Engine Models:** V2527-A5, CFM56-7B27  
**Affected Subsystems:** combustor  
**Compliance Interval:** Every 200 cycles  
**Supersedes:** None  
**Related Documents:** AMM-CFM56-72-41-00-200-801, AMM-V2500-72-41-00-200-801, AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001

## Summary

Mandatory combustion liner and fuel nozzle inspection on V2527-A5 and CFM56-7B27 engines following three cases of combustor hot-section burnthrough linked to low-cycle thermal fatigue and fuel nozzle coking. The AD requires a repetitive 200-cycle borescope inspection of the liner inner and outer barrels and of every fuel nozzle tip, rejection of liners with cracks beyond limits, and replacement of fuel nozzles that have exceeded 1,500 cycles since their last cleaning.

## Applicability

| Engine Model | Aircraft | Combustor Type | Fuel Nozzles |
|---|---|---|---|
| V2527-A5 | Airbus A320-232 | Annular, segmented liner | 20 airspray injectors |
| CFM56-7B27 | Boeing 737-800, 737-900ER | Single annular combustor (SAC) | 20 fuel nozzles |

CFM56-7B27 engines with the Tech Insertion combustor (CFM Service Bulletin 72-0835 incorporated) are also affected. CFM56-5B4 engines are not affected by this AD.

## Unsafe Condition

Thermal fatigue cracking of the combustion liner, aggravated by fuel nozzle coking, can propagate to a burnthrough of the liner and of the combustor case. A burnthrough can release hot gas into the engine nacelle, which could result in an engine fire, damage to the nacelle and pylon, and loss of the aircraft.

## Compliance

| Condition | Initial Inspection | Repetitive Inspection |
|---|---|---|
| Engines operating 8 or more cycles per day on average | Within 50 cycles after the effective date | Every 200 cycles |
| All other engines | Within 200 cycles after the effective date | Every 200 cycles |
| Engines approved for the extended interval (see Alternative Methods of Compliance) | Within 200 cycles after the effective date | Every 350 cycles |

## Mandatory Actions

1. Perform borescope inspection of combustion liner inner and outer barrels per manufacturer's AMM Chapter 72-41-00 (CFM56-7B27: AMM-CFM56-72-41-00-200-801; V2527-A5: AMM-V2500-72-41-00-200-801).
2. Inspect all fuel nozzle tips for coking and flow pattern anomalies using borescope with measurement capability.
3. Reject combustion liner with cracks exceeding 25 mm in length or any crack that has propagated through more than 50% of liner panel thickness.
4. Replace fuel nozzles with accumulated cycles greater than 1,500 cycles since last cleaning service.
5. Where carbon build-up on a nozzle tip obstructs more than 25% of the swirler exit, remove and replace the nozzle within 25 cycles regardless of cycles since cleaning.
6. Record EGT spread from the most recent take-off report together with the inspection results.

## Inspection Limits

| Feature | Location | Serviceable | Re-inspect in 50 Cycles | Reject |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Axial crack | Liner panel, dilution hole | Up to 12 mm | 12 to 25 mm | Greater than 25 mm |
| Circumferential crack | Cooling slot lip | Up to 10 mm | 10 to 25 mm | Greater than 25 mm |
| Crack depth | Any | Less than 25% of panel thickness | 25-50% | Greater than 50% |
| Burn or missing material | Liner panel | None | Not applicable | Any |
| Nozzle tip coking | Swirler exit | Up to 10% blocked | 10-25% | Greater than 25% (replace within 25 cycles) |

## Safety Warnings

CAUTION: Combustion section components retain elevated temperatures for up to 90 minutes after engine shutdown. Ensure engine total temperature (ITT) has returned to ambient before initiating borescope inspection. Fuel vapour may be present in the combustion section during the first 30 minutes after shutdown; maintain fire watch.

WARNING: Make sure the fuel shut-off valve is closed and the low pressure fuel valve is tagged before removing any fuel nozzle. Fuel at pressure can cause injury and fire.

## Terminating Action

Installation of a combustion liner of the improved cooling standard (IAE SB V2500-72-0612 for the V2527-A5; CFM SB 72-1189 for the CFM56-7B27), together with fuel nozzles of the anti-coking design, terminates the repetitive liner inspection. The fuel nozzle cleaning limit of 1,500 cycles remains in force.

## Alternative Methods of Compliance

Operators may apply to the responsible Flight Standards District Office for an extended interval of 350 cycles if they can demonstrate: (a) their operation averages fewer than 5 flight cycles per day per aircraft, (b) fuel nozzle cleaning is performed every 400 cycles, and (c) they have installed engine health monitoring that provides continuous EGT spread monitoring with automated alerts for spreads exceeding 15°C from baseline, as described in AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001.

## Reporting Requirements

Report any liner crack greater than 25 mm, any burn-through, and any nozzle with more than 25% blockage to the FAA Engine Certification Office and to the engine manufacturer within 10 days.

## Background and Technical Details

The FAA issues this Airworthiness Directive following the identification of a combustion liner thermal cracking pattern on V2527-A5 and CFM56-7B27 engines that has resulted in three cases of hot-section burnthrough in service. The cracking pattern is characterised by circumferential cracks initiating at the dilution hole cooling slots, driven by low-cycle fatigue from repeated thermal gradients during takeoff-climb-descent thermal cycling. The susceptibility is heightened on aircraft operating 8 or more flight cycles per day, a profile common in intra-European and intra-Asian short-haul operations.

Fuel nozzle coking, arising from insufficient cooling airflow through partially blocked nozzle feed passages, has been identified as a contributing factor in two of the three events. Coked nozzles produce asymmetric combustion temperature patterns that locally increase liner temperatures by up to 150°C above design conditions. This investigation has led to the dual requirement for both liner inspection and fuel nozzle condition assessment in this AD.

The 200-cycle inspection interval is conservatively set to detect early crack growth before propagation to a critical length. Operators may apply to the responsible Flight Standards District Office for an extended interval of 350 cycles if they can demonstrate: (a) their operation averages fewer than 5 flight cycles per day per aircraft, (b) fuel nozzle cleaning is performed every 400 cycles, and (c) they have installed engine health monitoring that provides continuous EGT spread monitoring with automated alerts for spreads exceeding 15°C from baseline.

Combustor distress often appears in trend monitoring before it is visible on borescope. A coked nozzle shifts the fuel distribution, so the fuel flow needed for a given thrust (the fuel flow ratio phi, Wf/Ps30) rises slightly and the circumferential spread of EGT probe readings widens. Neither change affects compressor parameters such as T30 or Ps30, which distinguishes combustor problems from HPC deterioration. In two of the three burnthrough events, the operator's records showed EGT spread increasing by more than 20°C over the preceding 300 cycles without an alert being raised, because the operator monitored mean EGT but not spread.

## Engine Health Monitoring Indicators

| Indicator | Sensor / Station | Signature of Combustor Distress |
|---|---|---|
| Fuel flow ratio | phi (Wf/Ps30) | Increase of more than 1.5% from baseline |
| Burner fuel-air ratio | farB | Small increase; validation only |
| EGT spread | Individual EGT probes | Spread more than 15°C above baseline |
| HPC exit temperature | T30 | No change (distinguishes from HPC) |
| HPT coolant bleed | W31 | May rise if liner cooling is disturbed |

## Definitions

- **Burnthrough:** A hole burned through the liner or case by combustion gas.
- **Dilution hole:** A hole in the liner admitting air to cool and mix the combustion gas.
- **EGT spread:** The difference between the highest and lowest individual EGT probe reading at a given condition.
- **Coking:** Carbon deposits formed from fuel in or on a fuel nozzle.

# AD-FAA-2024-1042 — Fan Blade Fracture Inspection

**Document Type:** Airworthiness Directive  
**Issuer:** Federal Aviation Administration (FAA)  
**Effective Date:** 2024-03-15  
**Affected Engine Models:** CFM56-7B27, CFM56-5B4  
**Affected Subsystems:** fan  
**Compliance Interval:** 150 cycles after effective date, then every 300 cycles  
**Supersedes:** None  
**Related Documents:** AMM-CFM56-72-21-00-200-801, SB-CFM-56-72-0228, AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001

## Summary

Mandatory fan blade root dovetail inspection following reports of fatigue crack initiation on CFM56-7B27 and CFM56-5B4 turbofan engines. The AD requires removal of all fan blades, fluorescent penetrant inspection (FPI) of the dovetail pressure and suction faces, a chord measurement at 50% span, a leading edge damage check, and reinstallation with new retaining clips and fresh dry-film lubricant.

## Applicability

| Engine Model | Serial Numbers | Aircraft | Fan Blade Part Numbers |
|---|---|---|---|
| CFM56-7B27 | All | Boeing 737-800, 737-900ER | 340-001-xxx series, all dash numbers |
| CFM56-5B4 | All | Airbus A320-214 | 338-002-xxx series, all dash numbers |

Fan blades installed on these engines, and spare fan blades, are affected. A spare blade must have a qualifying inspection on record before installation.

## Unsafe Condition

Fatigue cracking at the fan blade root dovetail, if not detected, can lead to fan blade separation. A separated fan blade can penetrate the fan case containment, damage the nacelle and the wing or fuselage, cause loss of thrust and, in the worst case, cause loss of the aircraft.

## Compliance

| Condition | Initial Inspection | Repetitive Inspection |
|---|---|---|
| Blades with more than 15,000 cycles since new | Within 150 cycles after the effective date | Every 300 cycles |
| Blades with 15,000 cycles or fewer | Before reaching 15,150 cycles since new, or within 150 cycles, whichever is later | Every 300 cycles |
| Blades inspected with FPI to sensitivity level 3/2 within the 100 cycles before the effective date | May be credited as the initial inspection | Every 300 cycles |

## Mandatory Actions

1. Remove all fan blades from the disc per AMM-CFM56-72-21-00-200-801. Record the position of each blade by serial number so that blades can be reinstalled in the same slot or re-balanced.
2. Clean the dovetails and perform fluorescent penetrant inspection (FPI) on blade root dovetail surfaces per CFM56 AMM 72-21-00.
3. Measure blade chord width at 50% span using calibrated digital caliper; reject if below 263.4 mm.
4. Inspect blade leading edge for impact damage exceeding 1.5 mm depth.
5. Inspect the fan disc dovetail slots for fretting, galling and coating loss; reject the disc per the limits in AMM-CFM56-72-21-00-200-801.
6. Apply new dry-film lubricant to the blade dovetail pressure faces and install new spacers.
7. Reinstall serviceable blades with new retaining clips P/N 1574M78G01; torque to 45 Nm ± 2 Nm.
8. Perform a fan trim balance if any blade was replaced or moved from its original slot.

## Inspection Limits

| Feature | Location | Reject Criterion |
|---|---|---|
| FPI linear indication | Dovetail pressure or suction face | Greater than 0.5 mm length |
| FPI indication | Dovetail edge or fillet | Any |
| Corrosion pit | Dovetail pressure face | Greater than 0.25 mm depth |
| Chord at 50% span | Airfoil | Less than 263.4 mm |
| Leading edge impact damage | Airfoil, 0-75% span | Greater than 1.5 mm depth |
| Dry-film lubricant wear-through | Dovetail pressure face | Base metal visible over more than 10% of contact area: re-lubricate; galling: reject |

## Safety Warnings

WARNING: Fan blade removal must be performed with engine fully shutdown and tagged out. Rotating components retain stored energy for up to 30 minutes after shutdown. Wear cut-resistant gloves rated to ANSI/ISEA 105 Level A4 when handling blades.

WARNING: Fan blades are heavy and have sharp edges. A CFM56-7B27 fan blade weighs approximately 5 kg. Use two persons to remove and install blades above shoulder height.

## Terminating Action

Replacement of all fan blades with blades of the improved dovetail coating standard (CFM Service Bulletin 72-0962, when approved) will constitute terminating action for the repetitive inspections of this AD. The FAA will publish the terminating action by superseding AD.

## Alternative Methods of Compliance

The Manager, Engine Certification Office, FAA, may approve alternative methods of compliance. An eddy current inspection of the dovetail with a demonstrated probability of detection equal to or better than FPI at 0.5 mm may be proposed as an alternative.

## Reporting Requirements

Report any crack indication, regardless of size, to CFM International and to the FAA within 72 hours. Include the blade serial number, cycles since new, cycles since last lubrication, aircraft operation (cycles per day) and FPI photographs.

## Background and Technical Details

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) issues this Airworthiness Directive (AD) following reports of fan blade fracture events on CFM56-7B27 and CFM56-5B4 turbofan engines installed on Boeing 737 Next Generation and Airbus A320-family aircraft. Investigation of two in-service events revealed fatigue crack initiation at the blade root dovetail pressure face, originating from a corrosion pit measuring approximately 0.8 mm diameter. Crack propagation through the titanium alloy (Ti-6Al-4V) blade root was observed to occur within 200 flight cycles of crack initiation under typical mixed short-haul mission profiles.

This AD requires operators to perform fluorescent penetrant inspection (FPI) of fan blade root dovetail surfaces at the intervals specified. Blades exhibiting any indication exceeding 0.5 mm length on the pressure face or suction face of the dovetail root must be removed from service immediately. Additionally, blade leading edge chord measurements must confirm that erosion has not reduced the airfoil chord below the minimum serviceable limit, as reduced chord width increases the aerodynamic loading per blade and accelerates fatigue crack growth. Operators must use CFM-approved inspection procedures in AMM Chapter 72-21-00 and record all results in the aircraft technical log with blade serial numbers.

The compliance cost is estimated at 6 man-hours per engine for initial inspection. Operators with blades already inspected within the preceding 100 cycles may credit that inspection against the initial compliance requirement provided the inspection was performed using FPI to sensitivity level 3/2 or better and the records are available for review by the responsible airworthiness authority. Non-compliance with this directive constitutes an unsafe condition that could result in uncontained fan blade separation and hull loss.

The investigation found that the corrosion pits formed where the dry-film lubricant on the dovetail pressure face had worn through. Once the lubricant is gone, the blade and disc contact directly, fretting removes the oxide layer, and moisture and chlorides trapped in the dovetail cause pitting. Both event engines had exceeded the recommended lubrication interval by more than 1,000 cycles. The inspection interval of 300 cycles therefore doubles as a lubrication interval: each time the blades are removed for inspection, the lubricant and spacers are renewed, which removes the conditions for pit formation.

The FAA considered whether trend monitoring could detect the cracks. It cannot reliably do so: a cracked blade does not change fan speed (Nf), bypass ratio or N1 vibration enough to be distinguished from normal scatter until the crack is close to critical length. Operators should still review N1 vibration trends as described in AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001, because a sudden vibration change after a bird strike or a foreign object event requires an unscheduled fan inspection irrespective of this AD's interval.

## Definitions

- **Dovetail:** The root of the fan blade that engages the matching slot in the fan disc.
- **FPI sensitivity level 3/2:** Fluorescent penetrant inspection using a level 3 penetrant and a level 2 developer per ASTM E1417.
- **Qualifying inspection:** An FPI inspection to the standard of this AD with blade-serial-number records.

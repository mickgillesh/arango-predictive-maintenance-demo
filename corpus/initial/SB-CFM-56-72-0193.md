# SB-CFM-56-72-0193 — HPC Water Wash Optimisation

**Document Type:** Service Bulletin  
**Recommended By:** CFM International  
**Effective Date:** 2024-02-14  
**Affected Engine Models:** CFM56-7B27, CFM56-5B4  
**Affected Subsystems:** HPC  
**Recommended Interval:** Every 200 cycles or when EGT margin loss exceeds 8°C from baseline  
**Supersedes:** SB-CFM-56-72-0141  
**Related Documents:** AD-EASA-2024-0312, AMM-CFM56-72-00-00-100-801, AMM-CFM56-7B-72-00-00-200-801, AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001

## Summary

Optimised two-phase compressor water wash programme for CFM56-7B27 and CFM56-5B4 engines, reducing the wash interval from 400 to 200 cycles for high-utilisation tropical operations to recover EGT margin loss from HPC fouling. The bulletin defines the wash fluid specification, the motoring sequence, a desalination rinse, a post-wash dry run and the EGT margin recording needed to judge the effectiveness of each wash.

| Engine Model | Aircraft | Operating Environment | Recommended Interval |

| CFM56-5B4 | Airbus A320 | Temperate | 400 cycles |

An operator may adopt the 200-cycle interval for its whole fleet to simplify planning. In all environments a wash is also recommended whenever EGT margin loss exceeds 8°C from the post-shop-visit baseline.

## Reason

Field data from 61 operators show EGT margin deterioration rates of 1.5°C per 100 cycles on average, rising to 2.8°C per 100 cycles for aircraft flying more than 8 cycles per day in hot, humid and coastal environments. Much of the early loss is HPC fouling: salt, sulphate and carbonaceous deposits on blade and vane surfaces that increase surface roughness and reduce compressor efficiency. Fouling is recoverable by washing. Unrecovered fouling also traps moisture and chlorides, which promote pitting corrosion of HPC blades and accelerate erosion damage of the type addressed by AD-EASA-2024-0312.

## Recommended Actions

1. Perform motoring wash per CFM56 AMM 72-00-00 Task 200 (AMM-CFM56-72-00-00-100-801) using deionised water conforming to ASTM D1193 Type IV, 90 litres minimum.
2. Perform desalination rinse using demineralised water at the completion of each wash.
3. Immediately following wash, perform a dry run at 25% N2 for 3 minutes to prevent compressor blade corrosion.
4. Record EGT margin before and 2 cycles after washing in engine health monitoring system.
5. If EGT margin recovery after the wash is less than 2°C, schedule an HPC borescope inspection per AMM-CFM56-7B-72-00-00-200-801 within 50 cycles to check for erosion, tip curl or tip clearance loss.
6. On CFM56-7B27 engines subject to AD-EASA-2024-0312, coordinate the wash with the 100-cycle borescope so that the wash is performed after the inspection images have been captured.

## Wash Procedure Parameters

| Parameter | Phase 1 — Wash | Phase 2 — Desalination Rinse | Dry Run |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fluid | Deionised water, ASTM D1193 Type IV | Demineralised water, conductivity below 1 µS/cm | None |
| Volume | 90 litres minimum | 45 litres minimum | Not applicable |
| Nozzle pressure | 3 bar ±0.2 | 3 bar ±0.2 | Not applicable |
| Fluid temperature | 40-60°C (above 5°C OAT) | 40-60°C | Not applicable |
| Engine condition | Dry motoring at starter limit, typically 18-22% N2 | Dry motoring | Idle run, 25% N2 or above |
| Duration | Two motoring cycles of 60 seconds with 5-minute cool-down | One motoring cycle | 3 minutes |

Below 5°C outside air temperature use a CFM-approved wash fluid with anti-freeze (60% deionised water, 40% isopropyl alcohol). Never use detergent wash fluid more often than every fourth wash, because detergent residue attracts dust if the rinse is incomplete.

## Background and Technical Details

CFM International issues this Service Bulletin to recommend an optimised water wash programme for operators of CFM56-7B27 and CFM56-5B4 engines experiencing EGT margin loss exceeding the typical rate of 1.5°C per 100 cycles. Field experience has demonstrated that operators conducting regular compressor washes recover an average of 4-6°C of EGT margin per wash event, with best results achieved when the wash interval is shortened to 200 cycles from the previously recommended 400-cycle interval.

The optimised wash programme specified in this SB uses a two-phase process: an initial wash with 90 litres of deionised water to dissolve salt deposits and mobilise carbonaceous fouling, followed by a desalination rinse to remove dissolved salts and prevent corrosion. The increased volume and two-phase process has demonstrated 35% better EGT margin recovery compared to the original single-phase wash procedure in back-to-back testing on five CFM56-7B27 engines operated on high-utilisation routes in tropical environments.

The importance of washing goes beyond fuel savings. EGT margin is the main reason a healthy CFM56 is removed for a shop visit: when the margin approaches zero on a hot day take-off, the engine must be removed even if no hardware is damaged. Recovering 4-6°C at every wash defers that removal by several hundred cycles. CFM's modelling of a 50-aircraft A320 or 737 fleet shows that moving from a 400-cycle to a 200-cycle wash interval extends average time on wing by 7-9% and reduces annual fuel burn by about 0.4%.

Washing is also a useful diagnostic. Because fouling is recoverable and erosion is not, the size of the EGT margin recovery after a wash separates the two causes of HPC deterioration. An engine that recovers less than 2°C after a correctly performed wash is likely to have blade erosion, tip curl or increased tip clearance, and CFM recommends a borescope inspection within 50 cycles. On CFM56-7B27 engines operating in the Middle East and North Africa, operators should expect this result more often, and should treat it as a cue to check compliance with AD-EASA-2024-0312.

Operators who already wash at intervals shorter than 200 cycles should continue doing so. There is no evidence that more frequent washing causes harm when the dry run in step 3 is performed, but omitting the dry run leaves water in the HPC rear stages, which has been linked to pitting corrosion of stage 7-9 blades on engines that are parked for several days after the wash.

Where the wash and the AD-EASA-2024-0312 borescope fall due within 20 cycles of each other, CFM recommends doing both at the same visit: the borescope first, so that the images show the blades in their as-operated condition, then the wash, then the dry run.

## Compliance Recording

Record each wash in the engine log book and in the operator's engine health monitoring system with the following data: date, aircraft registration, engine position and serial number, cycles since new, wash fluid used, volume, outside air temperature, EGT margin before the wash and EGT margin two cycles after. The pre/post comparison is what makes the wash useful as a diagnostic. Operators that cannot capture two-cycle post-wash data should at minimum record the next take-off EGT margin.

## Operator Questions

**Can the wash be performed with the engine hot?** No. Wait at least 30 minutes after shutdown, or until the HPC case is cool to the touch, so that the water does not cause thermal shock to the HPC case and blades.

**Does this SB replace the borescope required by AD-EASA-2024-0312?** No. The wash recovers fouling but does not detect or remove erosion damage. The AD remains mandatory on CFM56-7B27 engines regardless of wash frequency.

**What if EGT margin does not recover?** A recovery of less than 2°C indicates non-recoverable deterioration such as erosion, tip curl or increased tip clearance. Schedule an HPC borescope within 50 cycles and review the HPC exit temperature (T30) and fuel flow ratio (phi) trends for a step change.

**Is the wash effective on the CFM56-5B4 booster (LPC)?** Partially. The manifold sprays into the fan and booster first, so the LPC is cleaned as well, but the main benefit is in the HPC where deposits build up fastest.

## Effluent Handling

Wash effluent contains dissolved salts, sulphates, carbon and traces of oil. Collect it using the core drain bag set and dispose of it in accordance with local environmental regulations. Do not let effluent drain onto the apron.

## Definitions

- **EGT margin:** The difference between the certified EGT red line and the EGT recorded during a full-rated take-off, corrected to standard conditions.
- **Motoring wash:** A wash performed with the engine turned by the starter, without fuel or ignition.
- **Fouling:** Deposits on compressor airfoils that reduce efficiency and can be removed by washing.

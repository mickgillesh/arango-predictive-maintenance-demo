## Task Summary

This task gives the procedure to inspect the CFM56 single annular combustor (SAC) liner, dome and fuel nozzle tips with a borescope through the igniter and combustor borescope ports. It is the accomplishment procedure for the CFM56-7B27 inspection required by AD-FAA-2024-0583 and is used on condition for both models when trend monitoring shows a rising fuel flow ratio or a widening EGT spread.

## Job Set-Up

WARNING: OPEN AND COLLAR THE IGNITION CIRCUIT BREAKERS AND DISCONNECT THE IGNITION LEADS BEFORE YOU REMOVE THE IGNITER PLUGS. THE IGNITION EXCITER STORES A LETHAL CHARGE.

CAUTION: COMBUSTION SECTION COMPONENTS STAY HOT FOR UP TO 90 MINUTES AFTER SHUTDOWN. MAKE SURE THE ENGINE IS AT AMBIENT TEMPERATURE.

CAUTION: FUEL VAPOUR CAN BE PRESENT IN THE COMBUSTOR FOR 30 MINUTES AFTER SHUTDOWN. KEEP A FIRE WATCH AND DO NOT USE EQUIPMENT THAT CAN MAKE SPARKS.

1. Open the fan cowl doors and both thrust reverser halves.
2. Disconnect the ignition leads and remove the two igniter plugs.
3. Remove the combustor borescope port plugs C1 to C4.
4. Install the N2 hand-cranking adaptor.

## Procedure

1. Insert the videoscope through the 4 o'clock igniter port. Articulate to view the outer liner panels forward of the dilution holes.
2. Turn the N2 rotor slowly to bring each fuel nozzle into view. The nozzle tips are fixed; turning N2 helps to orient the probe using the HPT stage 1 nozzle guide vanes as a reference. Record the condition of each of the 20 nozzle tips, numbered clockwise from the top (aft looking forward).
3. For each nozzle, estimate the fraction of the swirler exit blocked by carbon. Measure deposit height where possible.
4. Inspect the outer liner panels: dilution holes, cooling slot lips, panel seams. Measure any crack with the reticle and estimate its depth from the visible crack width and edge condition.
5. Move to the 8 o'clock igniter port and repeat for the other half of the circumference.
6. Use ports C1 to C4 to inspect the inner liner barrel and the dome.
7. Look for local burn patterns, discoloured "hot streaks" on the liner and on HPT stage 1 nozzle guide vanes; a hot streak downstream of a nozzle usually indicates that nozzle is coked or has a distorted spray.
8. Compare findings with the limits below and with AD-FAA-2024-0583.

## Inspection Limits

| Feature | Serviceable | Re-inspect in 50 Cycles | Reject |
|---|---|---|---|
| Axial crack at dilution hole | Up to 12 mm | 12-25 mm | Greater than 25 mm |
| Circumferential crack at cooling slot | Up to 10 mm | 10-25 mm | Greater than 25 mm |
| Crack depth | Less than 25% of panel | 25-50% | Greater than 50% |
| Two cracks joining | Not permitted | Not applicable | Reject |
| Burn-through, missing material | None | Not applicable | Reject |
| Nozzle tip coking | Up to 10% blocked | 10-25% | More than 25% (replace within 25 cycles) |
| Thermal barrier coating loss on dome | Up to 15% of dome area | 15-30% | Greater than 30% |
| Hot streak on HPT nozzle guide vanes | Light discoloration | Erosion visible | Burn or missing material — HPT inspection required |

## Fault Isolation Notes

| Trend Observation (AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001) | Likely Finding | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Fuel flow ratio (phi) rising, T30 stable | Nozzle coking | Inspect nozzles; flow test and clean or replace |
| EGT spread widening by more than 15°C | Coked or blocked nozzle; liner distortion | Locate hot streak; replace nozzle |
| phi rising with T30 and EGT rising | Combined HPC and combustor deterioration | Do this task and the HPC borescope at the same visit |
| HPT coolant bleed (W31) rising | Cooling air disturbed by liner damage | Inspect liner panels and HPT nozzle guide vanes |

On the CFM56-7B27, combustor distress is the second most common driver of EGT margin loss after the HPC. Engines flagged with both HPC and combustor as driver subsystems should have this task and AMM-CFM56-7B-72-00-00-200-801 done at the same visit so that a single set of trend data is used to re-baseline both.

## Close-Up

1. Remove the videoscope; check that the tip is complete.
2. Install new gaskets and the borescope port plugs. Torque to 15-17 Nm.
3. Install the igniter plugs with new gaskets. Torque per AMM 74-21-00. Connect the ignition leads.
4. Remove the N2 hand-cranking adaptor and install the cover.
5. Close the circuit breakers and remove the tags.
6. Close the thrust reverser halves and fan cowl doors.
7. Do an ignition system functional test per AMM 74-00-00.

## Records

Record AD-FAA-2024-0583 compliance; the condition of each fuel nozzle by position; every liner crack with length, depth estimate and location; dome coating condition; hot streaks; and the EGT spread from the most recent take-off report. Retain images for the life of the liner.

## Background

The single annular combustor of the CFM56 burns fuel delivered by 20 fuel nozzles into a ring-shaped chamber between an outer and an inner liner. Air from the HPC enters through swirlers around each nozzle, through dilution holes and through cooling slots that lay a film of cool air along the liner wall. The liner is made of a nickel alloy with a thermal barrier coating, and runs in a gas stream well above its melting point; it survives only because of the cooling film.

Each flight heats and cools the liner, and the edges of dilution holes and cooling slots, where the metal is thinnest, crack first. A clean nozzle spreads fuel evenly, but when carbon builds up on the tip, the spray is distorted and a hot streak forms on the liner downstream. The hot streak raises the local metal temperature, speeds up crack growth and, if the crack reaches a cooling slot, can disrupt the cooling film and lead to a burnthrough. AD-FAA-2024-0583 followed three burnthrough events in which this sequence had occurred. Inspecting the nozzles and the liner together breaks the chain.

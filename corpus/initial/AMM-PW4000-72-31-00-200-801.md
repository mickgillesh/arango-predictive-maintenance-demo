This task gives the on-wing check of the PW4077D LPC variable stator vane (VSV) rigging, the actuator rod end play and the unison ring play, using the limits introduced by SB-PW-4000-72-0176.

## Job Set-Up

WARNING: THE VSV ACTUATOR IS MOVED BY FUEL PRESSURE. MAKE SURE THE ENGINE FUEL SUPPLY IS OFF AND THE EEC IS POWERED DOWN BEFORE INSTALLING THE RIG PIN.

1. Open the fan cowls and remove the VSV actuator access panel.
2. Install the VSV actuator rig pin to lock the actuator in the rig position.

## Procedure

1. Attach the protractor to the reference vane of each VSV stage (2, 3, 4, 5).
2. Read the stagger angle with the inclinometer. Record.
3. Compare with the rig position table; tolerance is ±0.5° per stage.
4. Remove the rig pin. Measure actuator rod end play with the dial indicator; limit 0.15 mm.
5. Measure unison ring radial play at four positions; limit 0.40 mm.
6. If any stage is out of tolerance, adjust the linkage turnbuckle per PW-72-VSV-RIG-003 and re-check.
7. If adjustment cannot restore tolerance, suspect bushing wear beyond 0.15 mm; plan an LPC module removal per SB-PW-4000-72-0176.
8. Do an engine ground run and check VSV schedule tracking on the EEC maintenance page.

| Feature | Limit |

## Fault Isolation Notes

| Trend Observation (AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001) | Likely Finding |
|---|---|
| T24 step change after maintenance | Linkage disturbed — re-rig |
| T24 gradual rise, NRf shift | Bushing wear — check play |
| LPC stall recorded in EEC fault log | Rigging out of tolerance, or HPC problem — check both |
| T24 and T30 both rising | LPC driving HPC inlet temperature — fix LPC first, then reassess HPC |

## Service Experience

Operator data from 140 on-wing rigging checks on PW4000-112 engines over three years show the following distribution of findings:

| Finding | Share of Checks | Typical Cause |
|---|---|---|
| All stages within ±0.5° | 61% | — |
| One stage out by 0.5-1.0° | 24% | Turnbuckle drift; bushing wear |
| Two or more stages out | 9% | Unison ring play; actuator wear |
| Actuator rod end play above 0.15 mm | 6% | Actuator wear — replace the variable stator vane actuator |

Most out-of-tolerance findings were on stage 2, which carries the highest aerodynamic load. Checks raised by a T24 trend alert found an out-of-tolerance stage three times more often than checks done after unrelated maintenance, which supports using trend monitoring to schedule this task. Where the HPC was also flagged as a driver subsystem, correcting the LPC rigging reduced the HPC exit temperature (T30) trend by 1-2°C, because the HPC inlet temperature fell as the LPC efficiency recovered; the remaining HPC drift should then be investigated with AMM-PW4000-72-00-00-200-801.

Variable stator vane actuators replaced on wing have had lead times of 20-30 days. Operators should hold at least one serviceable actuator per ten PW4077D engines.

## Close-Up

1. Remove all tools; install the access panel.
2. Close the fan cowls.

## Records

Record stagger angles per stage, play measurements, adjustments made and ground run tracking results.

## Background

The PW4077D LPC uses variable stator vanes on stages 2 to 5 to match the compressor to engine speed. The vanes are moved together by unison rings driven by a fuel-powered actuator. Wear in the vane trunnion bushings and in the linkage lets the vanes deviate from their commanded angle under aerodynamic load. SB-PW-4000-72-0176 tightened the bushing limit after a fleet survey found 18% of LPC modules out of rigging tolerance at shop visit. This on-wing check catches rigging drift between shop visits, before it causes an LPC stall.

## Task Summary

This task gives the inspection of the PW4077D wide-chord hollow titanium fan blades for leading edge erosion, impact damage and root condition, and the removal and re-lubrication of the fan blade roots. It is raised by trend monitoring when fan parameters (Nf, NRf, BPR, N1 vibration) are driver sensors.

## Job Set-Up

WARNING: LOCK THE FAN. WIND CAN TURN THE FAN AND CAUSE INJURY.

WARNING: EACH PW4077D FAN BLADE WEIGHS ABOUT 15 KG. USE THE REMOVAL FIXTURE AND TWO PEOPLE.

1. Install the fan lock.
2. For root lubrication, remove the spinner and the blade retainer ring.

## Procedure

1. Visual inspection, all blades in place: leading edge erosion, nicks, dents, tears; trailing edge damage; tip rub; mid-span condition.
2. Measure leading edge profile at 25%, 50% and 75% span with gauge PWA 85128.
3. Where impact damage is found, perform an eddy current check around the damage for cracks.
4. Root lubrication (every 1,500 cycles):
   1. Remove blades one at a time with the fixture; record slot positions.
   2. Clean the root and the disc slot; inspect for fretting, galling and cracks.
   3. Apply dry-film lubricant PWA 36571 to the root pressure faces; install new shims.
   4. Reinstall in the original slot.
5. Do a fan trim balance if any blade was replaced or moved.

## Inspection Limits

| Feature | Serviceable | Blend Repair | Reject |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leading edge erosion (profile loss) | Up to 0.5 mm | 0.5-1.5 mm | Greater than 1.5 mm |
| Leading edge nick, 0-60% span | Up to 1.0 mm | 1.0-2.5 mm | Greater than 2.5 mm |
| Dent, hollow blade panel | Up to 0.8 mm depth, no crease | Not applicable | Crease or greater than 0.8 mm |
| Crack (eddy current indication) | None | Not applicable | Any |
| Root fretting | Polished only | Not applicable | Galling or material transfer |

## Fault Isolation Notes

| Trend Observation (AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001) | Likely Finding |
|---|---|
| Nf/NRf rising for the same thrust, BPR falling | Leading edge erosion reducing fan efficiency |
| N1 vibration step change | Impact damage, lost shim, blade shifted |
| N1 vibration gradual rise | Root lubricant wear-through, uneven seating |
| Fan and HPC drift together | Shared ingestion environment — check both |

In the PW4077D fleet, the fan often appears as a secondary driver subsystem with the HPC. One critical engine in the fleet has HPC and fan as its driver subsystems. For engines like this, combine this task with the HPC borescope (AMM-PW4000-72-00-00-200-801) at the same visit and check stock of the fan blade set and fan disk assembly in advance, as both have long lead times.

## Service Experience

| Finding (110 inspections) | Share | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| No defect | 52% | — |
| Leading edge erosion 0.5-1.5 mm, blended | 23% | Mostly on engines operating in the Gulf region |
| Impact damage within blend limits | 14% | Bird strike or FOD; eddy current clear |
| Root lubricant wear-through | 7% | Found at 1,500-cycle lubrication |
| Blade rejected | 4% | Crease in hollow panel or crack at impact |

Engines whose trend data showed fan speed (Nf/NRf) rising for the same thrust had leading edge erosion beyond 0.5 mm in 70% of cases. Re-profiling the leading edge by blending recovered about 0.3% fuel burn. In all four blade rejections, the replacement had to come from a pooled spare because the fan blade set was out of stock locally; the aircraft was on the ground for between 3 and 9 days. For engines where the fan is a driver subsystem together with the HPC, inspect both at the same visit and confirm availability of a fan blade set and, where disc damage is suspected, the fan disk assembly before the aircraft is taken out of service.

## Close-Up

1. Install the retainer ring and spinner; torque per AMM 72-21-00.
2. Remove the fan lock.
3. Ground run; check N1 vibration below 1.0 unit at idle and at 80% N1.

## Records

Record blade positions, measurements, findings, blends performed, lubricant batch and trim balance results.

## Background

The PW4077D fan is 112 inches in diameter and produces most of the engine's thrust. Its hollow titanium blades are efficient but sensitive to leading edge shape: as erosion rounds and shortens the leading edge, fan efficiency falls, the fan must turn faster for the same thrust, and fuel burn increases. Erosion is fastest in sandy or dusty environments — the same conditions that erode HPC blades. Root lubrication prevents fretting and the corrosion pitting that can initiate cracks at the blade root.

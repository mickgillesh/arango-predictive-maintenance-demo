# SB-RR-TRENT700-72-0289 — HPT Disc Dovetail Slot Fretting Inspection and Mitigation

**Document Type:** Service Bulletin  
**Recommended By:** Rolls-Royce  
**Effective Date:** 2024-06-30  
**Affected Engine Models:** Trent 700  
**Affected Subsystems:** HPT  
**Recommended Interval:** At every HPT module removal, not to exceed 3,500 cycles  
**Supersedes:** None  
**Related Documents:** AD-EASA-2024-0756

## Summary

Enhanced HPT Stage 1 disc dovetail slot inspection using SEM surface sampling and tightened wear limits for Trent 700 engines, combined with MoS2 anti-fretting coating to reduce fretting wear rate by 55% on high-cycle short-haul operations.

## Applicability

| Engine Model | Aircraft | Module |
|---|---|---|
| Trent 772B-60, 772C-60, 768-60 | Airbus A330 | HPT stage 1 disc |

## Recommended Actions

1. At HPT module removal, inspect all HPT Stage 1 disc dovetail slots for fretting wear using scanning electron microscope (SEM) analysis of surface samples per RRTM NDT-72-HPT-SEM-001.
2. Measure dovetail slot width at root using calibrated gauging; reject disc if width exceeds nominal by more than 0.08 mm.
3. Apply RR-approved anti-fretting coating (MoS2 burnished layer) to dovetail slot surfaces before reinstalling blades.
4. At blade installation, verify blade-disc contact pattern using marking compound per CMM 72-50-00.
5. Record SEM findings and slot width measurements in the disc life record.

## Limits

| Feature | Serviceable | Reject |
|---|---|---|
| Slot width increase at root | Up to 0.08 mm | Greater than 0.08 mm |
| SEM fretting debris layer thickness | Up to 15 µm | Greater than 15 µm — further NDT |
| Crack under debris (SEM) | None | Any |
| Contact pattern | 70% or more of pressure face | Less than 70% — re-seat or investigate |

## Special Tools Required

- Scanning electron microscope with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (SEM/EDS) capability
- Dovetail slot width gauge P/N RR-T7-DOVE-GAUGE-003
- MoS2 anti-fretting burnishing kit P/N RR-MOLY-KIT-001

## Background and Technical Details

Rolls-Royce issues this Service Bulletin to recommend enhanced inspection and fretting mitigation measures for HPT Stage 1 disc dovetail slots on Trent 700 engines. Fretting wear at the blade-disc dovetail interface has been identified as a growing maintenance concern on Trent 700 engines accumulating more than 3,000 cycles, driven by the thermal cycling characteristic of short-haul operations which produces repeated relative micro-motion between the blade root and disc slot under the combined effects of centrifugal loading and thermal differential expansion.

Fretting produces a characteristic debris pattern (fretting oxide or fretting corrosion products) at the contact interface, which both increases the local surface stress concentration and, in the case of titanium alloy materials like the Trent 700 HPT disc, can mask crack initiation beneath the fretting debris layer. Rolls-Royce NDT research has demonstrated that standard visual inspection cannot reliably detect fretting debris or the cracks it masks; SEM surface sampling provides the necessary resolution to distinguish fretting debris from innocuous oxide contamination and to reveal the surface topography underlying the debris layer.

The MoS2 burnished layer acts as a solid lubricant at the blade-disc interface, reducing the friction coefficient and therefore the shear stress that drives fretting. In rig tests simulating 3,000 short-haul cycles, coated slots showed 55% less fretting wear than uncoated slots. The fretting mechanism is similar to the one addressed on Trent 700 fan blades by AD-EASA-2024-0756, where centrifugal and bending loads at the blade root drive micro-motion; operators may find it useful to coordinate the two programmes when planning shop visits.

## Definitions

- **SEM:** Scanning electron microscope.
- **EDS:** Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy, used with SEM to identify elements in surface deposits.
- **MoS2:** Molybdenum disulphide, a solid lubricant.

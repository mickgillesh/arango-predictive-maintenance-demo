## Task Summary

This task gives the borescope inspection of the five-stage V2527-A5 low pressure turbine (LPT): nozzle guide vanes, blades, tip shrouds and the abradable tip seals. It is raised when LPT exit temperature (T50) or LPT cooling bleed (W32) are driver sensors in the trend monitoring programme.

## Job Set-Up

WARNING: TAG OUT THE ENGINE AND OPEN THE FADEC AND IGNITION CIRCUIT BREAKERS.

CAUTION: LET THE LPT COOL TO AMBIENT TEMPERATURE BEFORE YOU REMOVE THE PLUGS.

1. Open the C-ducts.
2. Install the fan turning tool.
3. Remove LPT borescope plugs P1 to P3.

## Procedure

1. Through port P1, inspect the stage 3 nozzle guide vanes (first LPT stage on the V2500 numbering) and stage 3 blades. Turn the fan slowly and inspect every blade.
2. Record tip shroud condition, interlock wear, airfoil burns, nicks and cracks.
3. Articulate to the tip seal; estimate abradable seal wear depth and look for rub tracks.
4. Through ports P2 and P3, repeat for the remaining LPT stages.
5. Note any pattern of distress that follows a circumferential sector — this may originate in the HPT or the combustor.

## Inspection Limits

| Feature | Serviceable | Re-inspect in 500 Cycles | Reject |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blade crack | None | Not applicable | Any |
| Tip shroud interlock wear | Up to 0.4 mm | 0.4-0.6 mm | Greater than 0.6 mm |
| Tip shroud crack | Up to 2 mm | 2-4 mm | Greater than 4 mm or at interlock |
| Tip seal wear depth | Up to 1.0 mm | 1.0-1.6 mm | Greater than 1.6 mm |
| NGV trailing edge erosion | Up to 1.5 mm | 1.5-2.5 mm | Greater than 2.5 mm |
| Airfoil burn | None | Not applicable | Any |

## Fault Isolation Notes

| Trend Observation (AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001) | Likely Finding | Action |
|---|---|---|
| T50 rising, W32 rising, T30 stable | LPT tip seal wear | This task |
| T50 rising with T30 and Nc rising | HPC deterioration shifting load downstream | HPC inspection first |
| T50 rising with W31 rising | HPT distress | AMM-V2500-72-51-00-200-801 first |
| N1 vibration increasing | Shroud damage or blade loss | This task; check fan balance |

In the operator's V2527-A5 fleet, LPT usually appears as a secondary driver subsystem together with HPC. In most of those cases the HPC is the root cause and the LPT hardware is in good condition. Inspect the HPC first and use this task to confirm.

## Service Experience

| Finding (48 inspections) | Share | Typical Driver Sensors |
|---|---|---|
| LPT within limits — trend explained by HPC | 46% | T30, Nc, T50 |
| Tip seal wear 1.0-1.6 mm | 27% | T50, W32 |
| Tip shroud interlock wear above 0.4 mm | 13% | T50, N1 vibration |
| NGV trailing edge erosion | 10% | T50 |
| Blade damage | 4% | N1 vibration |

Nearly half of the LPT inspections on the V2527-A5 found the LPT within limits; in those cases the T50 rise was explained by HPC deterioration found during AMM-V2500-72-00-00-200-802. This is why the fault isolation notes recommend inspecting the HPC first. When true LPT deterioration was found, it was usually tip seal wear on engines beyond 8,000 cycles since the last LPT shop visit. LPT disks and LPT blade sets stage 1-4 are long-lead items; the LPT blade set has been out of stock at times, so an LPT finding requiring blade replacement should trigger an immediate parts check.

## Close-Up

1. Remove the videoscope; check the tip.
2. Install the plugs with new seals and high-temperature anti-seize; torque per AMM 72-00-00.
3. Remove the fan turning tool.
4. Close the C-ducts.

## Records

Record the reason for inspection, driver sensors, findings per stage, seal wear estimates and the next action. Feed the outcome back to the trend monitoring programme.

## Background

The V2527-A5 LPT has five stages and drives the fan and LPC through the LP shaft. Like other LPTs it relies on shrouded blades running against abradable seals to control tip leakage. Seal wear increases leakage, reduces LPT efficiency and raises EGT. LPT deterioration is gradual and rarely the primary reason for a V2500 shop visit, but it adds to EGT margin loss late in an engine's run.

Because T50 sits at the LPT exit, it reflects deterioration in every module upstream as well as in the LPT itself. Good fault isolation therefore depends on the other parameters: a rise in T50 with a rise in T30 points upstream to the HPC; a rise with W31 points to the HPT; only a rise with W32 and stable upstream parameters points to the LPT itself.

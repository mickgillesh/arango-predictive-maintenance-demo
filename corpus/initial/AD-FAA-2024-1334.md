# AD-FAA-2024-1334 — Fuel Nozzle Coking and Primary Combustor Zone Inspection

**Document Type:** Airworthiness Directive  
**Issuer:** Federal Aviation Administration (FAA)  
**Effective Date:** 2024-06-17  
**Affected Engine Models:** CF6-80C2  
**Affected Subsystems:** combustor  
**Compliance Interval:** Every 350 cycles  
**Supersedes:** None  
**Related Documents:** AD-FAA-2023-2156, SB-GE-CF6-72-0451

## Summary

Mandatory fuel nozzle flow testing and combustor dome tile inspection on CF6-80C2 engines following five cases of combustor dome tile loss linked to fuel nozzle coking from low-freeze-point polar fuel blends.

## Applicability

| Engine Model | Aircraft | Fuel Nozzles |
|---|---|---|
| CF6-80C2 (all dash numbers) | Boeing 747-400, 767; Airbus A300-600, A310 | 30 |

This AD does not apply to the CFM56, V2500 or PW4000 families, although AD-FAA-2024-0583 addresses a related coking mechanism on V2527-A5 and CFM56-7B27 engines.

## Unsafe Condition

Fuel nozzle coking creates locally rich mixture zones that overheat the combustor dome tiles. Tile loss can lead to further combustor damage, a hot streak onto the HPT, and in the worst case a combustor case burnthrough.

## Mandatory Actions

1. Remove all 30 fuel nozzles per GE CF6-80C2 AMM 73-11-00.
2. Flow-test each nozzle per GE procedure FT-CF6-FN-003; reject nozzles with flow variation exceeding ±4% from nominal or with pattern deviation.
3. Perform borescope inspection of primary combustion zone for spalling of thermal barrier coating on combustor dome tiles.
4. Reject dome tiles with TBC spallation exceeding 20% of tile surface area.
5. Reinstall serviced nozzles with new carbon seals P/N 1502M92G03.

## Limits

| Feature | Serviceable | Reject |
|---|---|---|
| Nozzle flow variation | Within ±4% | Outside ±4% |
| Spray pattern | Symmetric | Any streak or void |
| Dome tile TBC spallation | Up to 20% of tile area | Greater than 20% |
| Dome tile crack | Up to 10 mm | Greater than 10 mm or missing material |

## Special Tools Required

- GE fuel nozzle flow test bench P/N CF6-FNB-003 or approved equivalent
- Borescope, rigid, 6 mm diameter, angled access, 1080p
- Torque wrench, range 40-120 Nm, calibrated within 12 months

## Safety Warnings

WARNING: Fuel system components must be handled in a clean, spark-free environment. All fuel nozzle maintenance must be performed in a fuel system workshop compliant with NFPA 30. Personnel must wear anti-static grounding straps during fuel nozzle removal and installation. Ensure fuel supply is isolated and depressurised before removing any fuel system component.

## Background and Technical Details

The FAA issues this Airworthiness Directive to address fuel nozzle coking and combustor dome tile spallation on General Electric CF6-80C2 turbofan engines, following the identification of five cases of combustor dome tile loss in service over an 18-month period. Investigation determined that coking of the 30 fuel nozzles in the annular combustor creates locally rich mixture zones that elevate dome tile temperatures by 80-120°C above design intent, accelerating thermal fatigue and TBC spallation on the affected tiles.

The coking phenomenon is associated with the use of low-freeze-point fuel blends optimised for ultra-long-range polar routing, which are increasingly common as more CF6-80C2 operators have adopted polar routings to reduce flight times. These fuels have a higher aromatic content and slightly different volatility characteristics compared to conventional Jet-A, which affects the spray atomisation pattern and can promote carbon deposition in nozzle passages at low-power conditions such as descent and approach when nozzle temperatures are insufficient for self-cleaning.

The required inspection interval of 350 cycles is based on the observed coking accumulation rates for CF6-80C2 operators using low-freeze-point fuels for more than 40% of flights. Operators who can demonstrate through fuel purchase records that fewer than 40% of their CF6-80C2 flight cycles use low-freeze-point fuel specifications may apply to the FAA Aircraft Certification Office for an extended inspection interval of 600 cycles. GE Aviation recommends that all operators install its latest fuel nozzle design P/N 1502M92G06, which incorporates enlarged purge holes to reduce coking susceptibility; this upgrade is available through the GE On-Wing Support programme.

Where the HPT module is removed to address hot-section damage downstream of a coked nozzle, operators should combine the work with the HPT disc inspection required by AD-FAA-2023-2156. Operators incorporating the LPC exit guide vane modification of SB-GE-CF6-72-0451 at a fan module visit may also schedule the nozzle flow test at the same time.

## Definitions

- **Dome tile:** A replaceable, coated panel lining the front (dome) of the combustor.
- **Low-freeze-point fuel:** Jet fuel with a freezing point below the Jet-A specification, used on polar routes.

# SB-CFM-56-72-0228 — Fan Frame Strut Fatigue Inspection Enhancement

**Document Type:** Service Bulletin  
**Recommended By:** CFM International  
**Effective Date:** 2024-05-19  
**Affected Engine Models:** CFM56-7B27  
**Affected Subsystems:** fan  
**Recommended Interval:** At next fan frame access, not to exceed 5,000 cycles  
**Supersedes:** None  
**Related Documents:** AD-FAA-2024-1042, AMM-CFM56-72-21-00-200-801, AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001

## Summary

Enhanced fan frame outer bypass duct strut inspection procedure for CFM56-7B27 engines, adding eddy current inspection of the strut trailing edge fillet radius to detect fatigue cracks initiated by fretting wear, with anti-fretting compound application to extend crack initiation life by 800-1,200 cycles.

## Applicability

| Engine Model | Serial Numbers | Fan Frame Part Numbers | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| CFM56-7B27 | All | 340-150-101-0 through 340-150-109-0 | All strut positions; positions 4 and 5 most affected |
| CFM56-7B27/3 | All | 340-150-110-0 | Same strut design |

CFM56-5B4 engines are not affected. The CFM56-5B fan frame uses a different strut-to-frame attachment that does not exhibit the fretting mechanism described in this bulletin.

## Reason

Fatigue cracking has been found at the trailing edge fillet radius of fan frame outer bypass duct struts on four CFM56-7B27 engines removed for unrelated maintenance. The cracks were in a location not covered by the existing visual inspection. If a crack were to propagate, the strut could lose load-carrying capability. The fan frame is a primary structure that carries fan and core loads to the engine mounts; however, the frame is designed with redundant load paths and a single cracked strut does not create an immediate safety concern. For this reason CFM has issued a service bulletin, not an AD. The competent authorities have been informed and may mandate the bulletin if further findings occur.

## Recommended Actions

1. Inspect fan frame outer bypass duct struts for fatigue cracking per enhanced inspection procedure CFM56-7B AMM 72-21-00 Rev 12.
2. Strut inspection now requires eddy current inspection of the strut trailing edge fillet radius in addition to visual inspection.
3. Reject struts with any eddy current indication exceeding 0.3 mm depth equivalent.
4. If struts are found serviceable, apply anti-fretting compound Molykote D321-R to strut-to-frame interfaces before reinstalling cowl access panels.
5. Combine this inspection with the fan blade removal required by AD-FAA-2024-1042 where possible, because the fan blades must be removed to gain access to the inner strut fillets at positions 4 and 5.
6. Record strut positions inspected, eddy current results and compound application in the engine records.

## Background and Technical Details

CFM International issues this Service Bulletin to enhance the fan frame strut inspection procedure for CFM56-7B27 engines following the discovery of fatigue cracking in fan frame outer bypass duct struts on four engines removed for unrelated maintenance. The cracks were located at the strut trailing edge fillet radius, a location not previously included in the standard visual inspection scope. Visual inspection of these areas is limited by accessibility and the tendency for the crack to initiate at the sub-surface of the radius before breaking to the surface.

The root cause investigation identified that fretting between the strut and the fan frame mounting interface, driven by vibration-induced micro-slip under high-thrust operation, is abrading the surface protection and creating stress concentration points at the fillet radius. The fretting is most prevalent at the outer lower struts (positions 4 and 5) which experience the highest loading from fan thrust axis forces. Cycle-to-crack initiation at the identified severity of fretting has been calculated at 4,200-5,100 cycles, explaining why the cracks are presenting on engines in the 4,500-6,000 cycle range.

The enhanced inspection procedure adds eddy current inspection of the trailing edge fillet radius, providing the surface and sub-surface sensitivity necessary to detect cracks at an early stage. The eddy current method can detect cracks of 0.3 mm depth and above at this location with a probability of detection greater than 90% for qualified inspectors using the specified equipment. CFM recommends that operators adopt this enhanced inspection as part of their standard maintenance programme. The addition of anti-fretting compound at the strut-to-frame interface is expected to reduce fretting severity and extend the interval before crack initiation by an estimated 800-1,200 cycles based on laboratory accelerated fretting tests.

The vibration that drives the fretting comes mainly from fan imbalance. Engines with a history of high N1 vibration — from fan blade damage, uneven blade lubrication, or an incorrect trim balance — showed fretting damage two to three times deeper than engines with low vibration. CFM therefore recommends that operators review N1 vibration trends as described in AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001 and prioritise this inspection on engines whose vibration has exceeded 2.5 units at any time in the last 2,000 cycles. Keeping the fan well balanced after the blade inspections required by AD-FAA-2024-1042 is the most effective long-term mitigation.

CFM is designing a revised strut-to-frame attachment with a bonded wear pad that will remove the fretting interface. The new design is expected to be available for incorporation at shop visit from late 2025 and will be published as a separate service bulletin. Until then, the enhanced inspection and the anti-fretting compound are the recommended means of managing the condition.

## Definitions

- **Fan frame:** The structural frame behind the fan that carries fan and core loads and supports the outer bypass duct struts.
- **Fretting:** Wear caused by small oscillating relative motion between two contacting surfaces under load.
- **EDM notch:** A calibration defect machined by electrical discharge machining to a known depth.

## Task Summary

This task gives the procedure to inspect the nine stages of the CFM56-7B27 high pressure compressor (HPC) with a borescope through the HPC case borescope ports. It is used for the repetitive stage 3-5 inspection required by AD-EASA-2024-0312, for on-condition inspections after a bird strike, ingestion event or compressor stall, and to investigate an EGT margin or HPC exit temperature (T30) trend alert raised by the engine condition trend monitoring programme.

## Job Set-Up

WARNING: MAKE SURE THAT THE ENGINE START LEVER IS IN THE CUTOFF POSITION, THE START SWITCH IS TAGGED "DO NOT OPERATE" AND THE IGNITION AND FADEC CIRCUIT BREAKERS ARE OPEN AND COLLARED. IF THE ENGINE MOTORS WITH A BORESCOPE IN THE GAS PATH, INJURY AND DAMAGE TO EQUIPMENT CAN OCCUR.

CAUTION: LET THE ENGINE COOL FOR A MINIMUM OF 45 MINUTES AFTER SHUTDOWN BEFORE YOU REMOVE BORESCOPE PLUGS. HOT COMPONENTS CAN CAUSE BURNS AND CAN DAMAGE THE BORESCOPE OPTICS.

1. Open the fan cowl doors and the left thrust reverser half per AMM 71-11-00.
2. Remove the accessory gearbox hand-cranking pad cover and install the N2 hand-cranking adaptor 856A1128G02.
3. Identify the HPC borescope ports S1 to S9 on the HPC case. Ports S3, S4 and S5 are at the 2 o'clock position (aft looking forward) and are the ports used for the AD-EASA-2024-0312 inspection.
4. Remove the borescope port plugs for the stages to be inspected. Discard the gaskets. Put the plugs in a clean, labelled container.

## Procedure

1. Insert the rigid borescope through port S3 and position the view on the stage 3 blade leading edges at approximately 50% span.
2. Turn the N2 rotor slowly with the hand-cranking adaptor. Count the blades as they pass; stage 3 has 60 blades. Mark the first blade inspected using the blade with the locking feature as the reference.
3. For each blade, examine:
   - the leading edge for erosion, nicks, dents and coating loss;
   - the tip for curl, burrs, rub marks and missing material;
   - the airfoil surfaces for cracks, with particular attention to the leading edge at 25-40% span where fatigue cracks initiate from erosion notches;
   - the platform for corrosion and fretting.
4. Measure any erosion using the borescope measurement function (stereo or shadow probe). Record the deepest point for each blade that shows base metal.
5. Capture at least one image per blade at 1080p resolution. Name images by engine serial number, stage and blade number.
6. Repeat steps 1 to 5 for stage 4 (port S4, 68 blades) and stage 5 (port S5, 76 blades).
7. If a full inspection is required, repeat for stages 1, 2 and 6 to 9 using the articulating videoscope where line of sight from the rigid probe is not possible.
8. Measure stage 5 tip clearance per AMM 72-31-00-290-802.
9. Compare the findings with the limits table below and with AD-EASA-2024-0312.

## Inspection Limits

| Stage | Feature | Serviceable | Serviceable — Reduced Interval (50 cycles) | Reject |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | Leading edge nicks | Up to 0.5 mm | 0.5 to 0.8 mm | Greater than 0.8 mm |
| 3-5 | Leading edge erosion | Up to 0.9 mm | 0.9 to 1.2 mm | Greater than 1.2 mm |
| 3-5 | Tip curl | Up to 0.5 mm | 0.5 to 0.8 mm | Greater than 0.8 mm |
| 1-9 | Cracks | None | Not applicable | Any |
| 6-9 | Tip rub, burr height | Up to 0.3 mm | 0.3 to 0.5 mm | Greater than 0.5 mm |
| 6-9 | Corrosion pitting | Light, no depth measurable | Not applicable | Measurable pit depth |
| 5 | Tip clearance | 0.6 mm or greater | Not applicable | Less than 0.6 mm |

If any blade is at reject limits, the engine must not be released for further flight. Raise a deferred defect only where a ferry flight has been approved by the competent authority.

## Fault Isolation Notes

The HPC is the most common driver of performance deterioration on the CFM56-7B27. The trend monitoring signatures in AMM-GEN-77-00-00-ECTM-001 help the inspector know what to look for:

| Trend Observation | Likely HPC Finding | Inspection Emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| T30 rising, EGT margin falling, recovers after wash | Fouling | Confirm deposits; wash per SB-CFM-56-72-0193 |
| T30 rising, EGT margin falling, no recovery after wash | Erosion or tip clearance growth | Stages 3-5 erosion; stage 5 tip clearance |
| Sudden T30 step and N2 (Nc) step after a stall event | Blade damage or liberation | Full stages 1-9 including downstream damage |
| Ps30 falling at constant N2 with high bleed enthalpy (htBleed) | Seal or bleed leakage | Stages 6-9 seals; bleed valves |
| Rising fuel flow ratio (phi) with stable T30 | Not HPC — check combustor | Refer to AMM-CFM56-72-41-00-200-801 |

## Close-Up

1. Remove the borescope and examine the probe tip to make sure that no part of it was lost in the engine.
2. Apply anti-seize compound CP2101 to the threads of the port plugs. Install new gaskets 1873M51P02 and install the plugs. Torque to 15-17 Nm.
3. Remove the N2 hand-cranking adaptor and install the cover. Torque per AMM 72-60-00.
4. Remove the tags and close the circuit breakers.
5. Close the thrust reverser and fan cowl doors per AMM 71-11-00.
6. Do a dry motor leak check per AMM 71-00-00 if any plug was removed from a port that also carries a bleed or sensor fitting.

## Records

Record the following in the engine log book and the maintenance tracking system: task number, AD reference where applicable, date, engine serial number, cycles since new and since last shop visit, stages inspected, the maximum erosion and tip curl for each stage, any blade at reduced-interval limits, the next due inspection, and the image archive reference. Retain images for 2 years per AD-EASA-2024-0312.

## Background

The CFM56-7B27 HPC is a nine-stage axial compressor with variable inlet guide vanes and variable stator vanes on stages 1 to 3. It raises the pressure of the core flow from the booster exit by a ratio of about 11:1. Stages 3 to 5 sit in the region where particulate matter that passes through the fan and booster still has enough momentum to erode leading edges, but the blades are already small and thin, so a small loss of material has a large effect on blade stiffness and on stage efficiency.

The HPC also drives the engine's thermodynamic health. A one-point loss of HPC efficiency increases T30 by about 3°C and EGT by about 6°C at take-off thrust. Trend monitoring therefore often detects HPC deterioration before a borescope is due, and this task is often triggered by a monitoring alert rather than by the calendar. Fleet data show that on a CFM56-7B27 fleet, more than half of the engines flagged for accelerated EGT margin loss have the HPC as their primary driver, typically together with a secondary contribution from the combustor or the low pressure turbine.

When a borescope finds reduced-interval erosion but no reject condition, the operator should wash the engine per SB-CFM-56-72-0193, re-baseline the trend, and plan the HPC refurbishment at the next shop visit. Where stage 3 or 4 blades are rejected, replacement of the HPC blade set stage 1-4 at a shop visit is normally required, because individual blade replacement in the front stages requires moment-weight matching that is not practical on wing.
